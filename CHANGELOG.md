# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/); the project does not yet follow semantic versioning in the strict sense, since its interface is the pinned specification rather than an API. Every entry names the decision records (`docs/adr/`) and the guide chapter (`docs/guide/`) that describe it; the trust model that says what is proved, tested and trusted is in the README.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- The development process (`docs/process.md`): the planning structure, the Definitions of Ready and Done, the workflow and the gate, the rules of proof, measurement and testing, the review checklist, the labels and the releases. Issue forms for blocks, tasks and bugs, a pull request template, `CONTRIBUTING.md`, `CODEOWNERS`, and Dependabot for GitHub Actions. No code change.
- Three premises of upstream's Lean proofs are checked, with proved postconditions (ADR 0064, Accepted). The reader refuses a circuit whose assertion shares its wire (`Not_Isolated`) or is read by the layer above (`Not_Dead_End`). The verifier and the prover refuse a statement whose first public input is not 1 when the circuit has public inputs (`Public_Input_Not_One`, `Input_Not_One`). All circuits shipped by upstream pass. One negative vector for each new status.
- `docs/guide/lean-correspondence.md`: the protocol theorems upstream proves in Lean for the v7 verifier (at d9d8d6a: 115-bit soundness, zero knowledge within 2^-127), their premises, and a row-by-row correspondence with the units and Gold contracts of this project; the trust model points to it.
- `Rfft_P256`, the Reed-Solomon extension on P-256 as the production prover computes it: the middle product of `reed_solomon.rs` by the real FFT over F_p^2 of `rfft.rs` (half-complex storage, radix 4) with the reference's root of unity; the P-256 prover encodes its rows with it and keeps no Lagrange table (ADR 0057, corrected: F_p has no large roots of unity). `gen_rs_p256_vectors.py` and the P-256 cases of `fft_kat`.
- `Additive_Fft`, the Reed-Solomon extension on GF(2^128) by the additive FFT of Lin, Chung and Han, in the form of the production prover; the GF(2^128) prover encodes its rows with it and keeps no Lagrange table, P-256 kept the Lagrange encoder until `Rfft_P256` (chapter 8, ADR 0057). `gen_fft_vectors.py` and the harness `fft_kat`; the `config_invalid` case of `zk_prove.txt` moves to P-256, and a GF(2^128) case with more columns than the subfield holds is added.
- The mDoc production circuits of upstream, versions 7 and 6 with one attribute: archives, example proofs, witnesses and the extracted LFC2 circuits under `tests/vectors/upstream/mdoc/`, read by the independent `gen_mdoc_vectors.py` and loaded by `circuit_kat` (Phase D, ADR 0055 to 0059). `scripts/measure-mdoc.sh` measures the prover and the verifier on them, on macOS or Linux, with an optional profile per function.

### Changed

- The command-line build `proved` compiles `Pinned_Circuits` with its run-time checks, as every unit of `cli/src/` (ADR 0068 amended). The unit, outside the proof, was missing from the list of the project file. `scripts/check.sh cli` now reads the switches each unit was compiled with from its ALI file and fails if a unit of `cli/src/` lacks its checks or a unit of `src/` keeps them. The build passes `-s` to `gprbuild`, so that a change of switches recompiles the units it concerns: without it, an object directory from an earlier build kept `Pinned_Circuits` without its checks, and the new check caught it. The trust model says so.
- The assurance statements say what the proofs cover and no more. The circuit loader: its structure is proved, its decoding of the file is tested, its correspondence to Lean's circuit is checked by reading. The absence of run-time errors holds for the inputs that meet the preconditions, with a failed allocation, code outside SPARK and the termination of the samplers outside. Known-answer vectors are independent evidence, not a proof of the references. A circuit is executed safely and as its terms say, but what it computes is trusted. README, verification approach, trust model and Lean correspondence. No code change.
- The README no longer calls Longfellow ZK Google's: Google donated it in September 2026 to the Post-Quantum Cryptography Alliance, under Linux Foundation Europe, and upstream's repository is now longfellow-zk/longfellow-zk. The provenance lines of the vectors keep the name google/longfellow-zk, under which d5e6be7 was published. A new section, "About this project", states that the project is the author's personal project and that its views are the author's own, not those of the author's employer. No code change.
- The audience of the repository and the order of the work after the memory milestone (ADR 0081 and ADR 0082, Proposed). The repository is written for evaluators in a certification laboratory, with no expertise in cryptography or SPARK assumed; the introduction of the guide says so, and that the chapters are being revised. After the memory milestone come the mDoc layer, a milestone for the guide, the certification block, then M3, opened by the software optimisations in Gold. Phase D has two releases, 0.4.0 and 0.5.0. ADR 0055 and ADR 0080 amended. No code change.
- The memory milestone (ADR 0080, Proposed): the peak heap within twice upstream's, by the same measure, in Gold. First step: the verifier passes a layer's terms to `Bind_Quad` as a slice, by reference, where a function result copied the largest layer, and the command-line prover frees its provisional input buffers. On the proving machine the hash verification peaks at 401 MB instead of 515 MB, the hash proof at 621 MB instead of 645 MB. The contracts and the proofs of the CLI unchanged.
- The specification is draft-irtf-cfrg-longfellow-00, the CFRG version of draft-google-cfrg-libzk-03 with the same technical text, and upstream is longfellow-zk/longfellow-zk (ADR 0079, Proposed). The Lean correspondence follows upstream's main at 7bf8a6b: 116-bit soundness for the pair of mDoc circuits, witness indistinguishability, the ECDSA circuit, and the implementation obligations of `formal/AUDIT.md` against what this project proves. The guide, the trust model and the index are aligned with the code at the close of M2. No code change beyond comments.
- Milestone M2 closed (ADR 0078, Proposed): the Gold limit at a round trip of 8.13 s on the proving machine, against 5.83 s for upstream's generic code at the same processor target, with every contract proved. The gap, item by item, is scope, M3 items and code generation by the same algorithm. New page `docs/guide/performance.md`; ADR 0055 and ADR 0067 point to the record.
- Milestone M2, the Montgomery round of P-256 as upstream's CIOS step (ADR 0077, Proposed). `Round` makes T + a_i B and T + r p with `Mac_Row`, four 128-bit multiply-accumulates in place, instead of a row and three six-word carry chains. On the proving machine the product goes from 29.4 to 19.9 ns (throughput), the signature proof from 0.57 s to 0.50 s and its verification from 0.36 s to 0.30 s. The contracts unchanged, the proofs of the CLI unchanged byte for byte. Guide chapter 2.
- Milestone M2, the command line checks the circuit predicate once (ADR 0072 amended). `longfellow_prove` and `longfellow_verify` test `Well_Formed` explicitly after the load and refuse with `not_well_formed`, and no longer check it again at each call that passes the circuit, about four walks of the whole circuit. On the proving machine the hash proof goes from 4.86 s to 4.70 s. The contracts and the proofs unchanged. The trust model.
- Milestone M2, the field operations without call and finalisation overhead (ADR 0076, Proposed). `Add`, `Sub`, `Neg` and `Mul` of GF(2^128) are inlined. The P-256 bodies hold no `Big_Integer` temporaries any more: GNAT kept the finalisation of those of their `Proof_Only` assertions, and every `Add` and `Sub` ran a finaliser. The proofs of `Add_Mod` and `Sub_Mod` move into ghost lemmas, `Mont_Mul` closes through `Lemma_Mont_Close`, and the contracts do not change. The proofs of the CLI unchanged byte for byte. Guide chapter 2.
- Milestone M2, the prover's round evaluations take p (1) from the claim of the round, as upstream's `quad_round_poly` (ADR 0075, Proposed). `Round_Evals_Dense` takes two products per pair instead of three; its postcondition gives the reference p (P2) when the claim is the true sum, and upstream's formula for any claim. `Prove_Layer` tracks the claim as upstream does, and `prover_kat` checks it at the end of every layer, as upstream's assertion. About 6.9 M products less on the mDoc hash circuit. On the proving machine the hash proof goes from 5.32 s to 5.09 s. The proofs of the CLI unchanged byte for byte. ADR 0070 amended, guide chapter 7, the trust model.
- Milestone M2, the command line checks the identifier of a known circuit once (ADR 0074, Proposed). `Circuit_Reader.Read_Pinned` loads a circuit as `Read` does without recomputing its identifier, and states the identifier the caller gives. `Pinned_Circuits` in `cli/src/` lists the three mDoc circuits by the SHA-256 of their files and their identifiers; `longfellow_prove` and `longfellow_verify` load a listed file with `Read_Pinned` and any other with `Read`, and `--check-circuit-id` forces `Read`. `interop_kat` checks every entry against a full `Read`. On the mDoc hash circuit the identifier hashed 310 MB, about 2.2 s of each proof and verification on the proving machine. There, the hash proof goes from 7.52 s to 5.37 s and the hash verification from 4.82 s to 2.61 s. The proofs of the CLI unchanged byte for byte.
- Milestone M2, the verifier binds the quad of each layer as upstream's `bind_quad` (ADR 0073, Proposed). The kernels `Bind_G_T`, `Merge_Runs` and `Bind_H`, with the ghost package of ADR 0071, move to the new generic `Sumcheck_HQuad`, shared by the verifier and the prover. The new `Bind_Quad` is Gold against `EBQT_Ref`; `Bound_Quads` uses it within the eq tables and keeps its contract. On the mDoc hash circuit the verifier goes from 59.6 M to 51.3 M field products, and on the proving machine from 5.17 s to 4.90 s. `sumcheck_kat` compares `Bind_Quad`, `Eval_Bound_Quad_T` and `Eval_Bound_Quad` on every layer. Guide chapters 3 and 7, the trust model, lean-correspondence.
- Milestone M2, the prover instances of the command line no longer check the circuit predicate inside the proved bodies of `Sumcheck_Prover` and `Zk_Prover` (ADR 0072, Proposed). The check scanned the whole circuit once per layer since ADR 0071. The circuit is still checked on entry. Hash proof on the proving machine from 8.32 s to 7.53 s; the proofs of the CLI unchanged byte for byte. ADR 0068 amended, trust model.
- Milestone M2, the prover reuses the bound quad of its own sumcheck (ADR 0071, Proposed). `Sumcheck_Prover.Prove` returns the bound quad of each layer, and its postcondition proves it equal to the verifier's reference `BQ_Ref`, through an invariant on the coefficients kept round by round. `Verifier_Core` takes the bound quads as an input, with that equality as its precondition. The verifier computes them in the new pass `Bound_Quads`, Gold. On the mDoc hash circuit the prover goes from 143.1 M to 102.3 M field products; the verifier is unchanged. The proofs of the CLI are unchanged byte for byte. `prover_kat` compares the two values on every layer. Guide chapters 3 and 7, the trust model.
- Milestone M2, the sumcheck and Ligero kernels in upstream's form (ADR 0070, Proposed). `Bind` and `Bindeq` take one product where they took two, `Eq2_Table` starts its second table from alpha, the dense round evaluations take three products per pair instead of four, and the prover extends each witness row to dblock columns only for y_dot. The contracts are unchanged. The bodies reach the same references through the ring laws that `Field_Signature` now lists (commutativity, the unit, distributivity, the additive group), proved by each field, and the new generic `Ring_Laws`, instantiated on the hidden wrappers. The four limbs of a P-256 element are now scalar components, so equal limbs give equal elements; `Eq` and `Norm` leave `P256_Field`. The type predicate is `Canonical_Limbs` on the four limbs, its body hidden from the provers outside `Of_Limbs` and `Lemma_Canonical`. `fft_kat` checks that the extension to dblock is the prefix of the full one. Guide chapters 2, 3 and 7, the trust model.
- P-256 in Montgomery form, with the multiplication of upstream (ADR 0069, Proposed). An element stores x 2^256 mod p; `Mul` is the interleaved Montgomery method of `fp_generic.rs` with the reduction step special to p of `p256_64.rs`, Gold: the stored result is A B 2^-256 mod p. The Solinas reduction of ADR 0013 leaves the code. `From_Bytes`, `To_Bytes` and `Of_Natural` convert, and the new `From_Canonical` and `To_Canonical` serve the constants of `Field_P256` and `Rfft_P256` and the hex parsers of the command line and the harnesses. `Lemma_Mul_Assoc` is proved again in the new model. `Mul` from 230 to 50 ns on the author's laptop; the proofs of the CLI unchanged byte for byte. Guide chapter 2 and the trust model.
- The release build of the command-line programs relies on the proof of absence of run-time errors (ADR 0068, Proposed). By default the units of `src/` are compiled at `-O3 -gnatn` without run-time checks, and the units of `cli/src/`, outside the proof, keep theirs. `LONGFELLOW_CLI_BUILD` still selects the earlier `-O2` build (`o2`) or `-O3` with every check (`o3`). The two programs check the length of the label against the transcript before calling the library. On the proving machine, v7 mDoc, the round trip from 27.4 s to 22.5 s (the signature proof from 6.2 s to 3.6 s), the proofs unchanged byte for byte.
- Milestone M2, the multiplication in GF(2^128) (ADR 0065, Proposed). `Mul` is now the software multiplication of upstream's `generic.rs`: Karatsuba over three 64 by 64 carry-less products, each made of sixteen integer products of masked words, then the two folds of the reduction. The new unit `Clmul64` holds the products and shares the bit reversals of their high words among the three. `Mul` keeps its contract and is Gold against `Mul_Ref` through the 256-bit product. The bit-by-bit form stays as `Mul_Serial`, Gold as before, and `gf2_128_kat` compares the two on the vectors and on 200 000 random operands. The proofs are the upstream ones byte for byte. On the proving machine, v7 mDoc: the hash proof from 33 s to 12 s, the hash verification from 41 s to 18 s; the signature, on P-256, is unchanged. The trust model adds one assumption: the processor's integer multiplication takes a time independent of its operands (chapter 1).
- Milestone M2, the verifier's Lagrange tables (ADR 0066, Proposed). `Lagrange.Build` fills each table column by column, from prefix and suffix products, about 3 K multiplications per column instead of K squared. Its contract is unchanged: Gold against the Lagrange formula, through the associativity of the product. `Field_Signature` gains that law as a formal lemma, proved by each field: GF(2^128) against `Mul_Ref`, P-256 from its integer model. `P256_Field.Mul` returns its result in a normal form, stated in its postcondition, so that equal limbs give the logical equality `Same`; the trust model lists `P256_Field.Eq`, of the same kind as `Same`. The proofs are identical byte for byte. On the proving machine, v7 mDoc: the hash verification from 18 s to 6.5 s, the signature verification from 49 s to 2.8 s; the round trip is 27.8 s. ADR 0067 (Proposed) sets the next milestones: the Gold limit, compared with upstream's generic code, then a tested variant.
- `Sha256` compresses the whole blocks of its input in place and keeps the loops of `Compress` scalar and unrolled: 104 to 215 MB/s at `-O2` on the author's laptop, 309 MB/s at `-O3`. `transcript_kat` adds the one million 'a' of FIPS 180-2 in updates of 1000 bytes.
- Milestone M1, algorithmic parity with the production prover and verifier (ADR 0063, closed at about 126 s). The sumcheck uses the eq tables of Section 6.2 for the bound quad and for bind_g, computes the round polynomial in the dense form of `quad_round_poly`, and keeps the terms merged as `hquad.rs` does: the runs of equal hands merged after bind_g, the two corners of the bound hand merged at every round. Each fast kernel is Gold against its own ghost reference; the reference kernels stay, and the harnesses check that both agree. The proofs are the upstream ones byte for byte. On the proving machine, v7 mDoc: the hash proof from 6 min 27 s to 33 s, the signature proof from 42 s to 6 s, the hash verification from 2 min 2 s to 41 s.
- The input constraint is written straight into the Ligero terms in linear time, and `Input_Constraint` is Gold against the formula of Section 6.7; the per-layer values of `Eval_Circuit` are on the heap (ADR 0056). The prover and the verifier of the mDoc hash circuit now fit the default 8 MB stack, where they needed 64 MB, and its verification takes 24% less time. 17 002 checks proved.
- The README is a short front page, and the full trust model is `docs/guide/trust-model.md`, arranged by part of the system (ADR 0061, amending ADR 0003). No entry changed in meaning.
- The proof runs on demand: `scripts/remote-check.sh` runs `make check` on the proving machine for the pushed commit and posts the result as the commit status `prove`, which branch protection requires with `verify`; `--text-only` posts it when only text changed since the last proved commit or `main`, `--tree` checks a working tree without posting. The run on a commit is detached on the machine, which keeps the result and shuts itself down, so the developer's laptop may sleep; `--collect` picks up a run whose script was closed, and a second run is refused while one is in progress. The `prove` workflow and the self-hosted runner are withdrawn, and `make check` before a commit is optional (ADR 0062, amending ADR 0041 and 0060).
- The `prove` workflow's job limit is sixty minutes and the prover units are two proof partitions, `prover` and `ligero-prover`, nine in all (ADR 0054): the one partition of Phase C exceeded both the forty-five and the sixty minute limit on the hosted runner.
- The proof runs on the project's own proving machine on Google Cloud (ADR 0060; the workflow and the self-hosted runner were then withdrawn by ADR 0062, above): the `prove` workflow starts the VM and runs `make prove` as one job on its self-hosted runner, replacing the partition matrix on hosted runners; `scripts/remote-check.sh` runs `make check` there from the developer's working tree.

### Fixed

- The quadratic row of `Ligero_Prover.Commit` is built in its own procedure, `Quad_Fixed_Row`, so that its witness slots are proved zero in a small context: on the proving machine, whose cores are slower than the laptop's, the stepping assertions after `Commit_Row` in `Fixed_Rows` ran out of the 120-second timeout. No contract changed.

## [0.3.0] - 2026-10-19

Phase C: the prover. From a circuit, its inputs and a randomness source to the proof string of Section 7.5, in the reference's order of operations: the two proofs that google/longfellow-zk ships are reproduced byte for byte from their inputs and seeds.

### Added

- The prover units, generic over the field's transcript glue and over a `Sampling` instance that carries the randomness source (chapter 7; ADR 0049 to 0053): `Prover_Status`; `Simple_Rng`, the generator of the upstream reference test, and `Sampling` (the reference's samplers on a byte stream); `Proof_Writer` (the writers of Sections 7.3, 7.4 and 7.5, the run-length structure of the subfield encoding decided by position, Gold on the reader's rules `Well_Coded`); `Sumcheck_Prover` (the pads of Section 6.5, the round evaluations proved equal to the ghost transcription of `eval_round_poly`, the layers on the live transcript with the challenges recorded for the core, the quadratic triples); `Ligero_Prover` (the tableau of Section 4.3 on the heap, the Reed-Solomon encoder on all columns with its systematic part proved equal to the row, the commitment with nonces and `Merkle.Build`, the proof of Section 4.4 with the verifier's own challenge and absorption procedures); `Merkle.Open`, the batch opening of Section 4.1.3, Gold in the model of `Merkle.Verify`; `Zk_Prover`, the composition of `zk/prover.rs` with the validation statuses `Input_Length`, `Not_Satisfied`, `Not_In_Subfield`, `Config_Invalid` and `Exhausted`; the fixed projection of GF(2^128) on the coordinates of GF(2^16) (`GF2_128.Subfield_Coords`, sixteen masks) and the formal `To_Subfield_Bytes` of `Field_Signature`; the proof partition `prover` (4591 checks).
- The command-line prover `longfellow_prove` in `cli/` (ADR 0049, 0050): the field, the circuit file, the inputs file in the upstream JSON format, the label, the Ligero configuration, the output file; the operating system's generator by default (`Os_Random`, `/dev/urandom`, outside SPARK) and the upstream test generator behind `--seed`; exit status 0 written, 1 prover status, 2 usage or file error.
- Vectors and harnesses (ADR 0053): `gen_prover_vectors.py`, an independent Python transcription of the reference prover piece by piece (`writer.txt`, `sumcheck_prover.txt`, `ligero_commit.txt`, `zk_prove.txt`) and the masks of the projection in `gen_gf2_128_vectors.py`; the harnesses `writer_kat` (every proof of the repository rewritten identical) and `prover_kat` (the pads and padded sumcheck proofs, the commitments with their openings, the Merkle roots of the two upstream proofs reproduced, the complete proofs of the small circuits accepted by `Zk_Verifier`, the statuses, and the refusal of the proofs an unchecked prover makes of false inputs); the `cli` step reproduces `sha256_proof.bin` and `ecdsa2_proof.bin` byte for byte with `longfellow_prove` under the upstream seeds and verifies proofs under another seed and under the system source; `verify_proof_file.rs`, a test for the upstream crate that verifies a proof file of this project with the upstream verifier, run by hand.

### Changed

- 16671 checks proved in all (11967 at 0.2.0), eight proof partitions; the `prover` partition is now the heaviest and `make check` takes about an hour and a quarter.
- `Merkle`, `GF2_128` and `Field_GF2_128` gained the opening and the projection; `Field_Signature` gained one formal, so every instance of the sumcheck generics names `To_Subfield_Bytes`.

### Trust model

The operating system's generator is trusted for the zero-knowledge property of the proofs made without `--seed`; the shapes of the tableau are proved row by row and tested as a whole (ADR 0052, amended at block 4); completeness is tested, not proved. The README lists the entries.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-10-17

Phase B: the verifier takes the circuits the upstream compiler produces and accepts the proofs the upstream prover made for them, from their files, on both fields.

### Added

- The LFC2 circuit loader (`Uleb128`, `Circuit_Status`, `Circuit_Reader` generic over the sumcheck algebra of a field, instantiated for GF(2^128) and P-256; chapter 6; ADR 0046): the format as the upstream production reader at d5e6be7 defines it, read in two passes with the circuit allocated once on the heap, `Well_Formed` of the core established on the result and the identifier (`compute_id`) recomputed and checked; `Uleb128.Read` proved Gold on the decoded value, `Circuit_Reader.Read` Silver on arbitrary bytes with the ownership checks and Gold on the shape of its result; 584 checks in the new proof partition `circuits`.
- The working sets of `Zk_Verifier.Verify` and `Ligero_Verifier.Verify` in one heap allocation each, under SPARK's ownership rules, freed on every path (ADR 0047): the supported scale is the available memory, the stage 3 input constraint excepted.
- The release project `cli/` with the command-line verifier `longfellow_verify` (exit status 0 accepted, 1 refused, 2 usage) and the interop harness `interop_kat`; the step `cli` of `scripts/check.sh` and of the `verify` workflow.
- Vectors (ADR 0048): the two upstream circuits, inputs and proofs copied under `tests/vectors/upstream/circuits/` and pinned by SHA-256; `gen_circuit_vectors.py`, an independent LFC2 reader and writer whose `compute_id` must equal the identifiers the upstream files store; `circuit_summary.txt`, `circuit_lfc2.txt` (the Appendix B.3 circuits in two encodings, 28 malformed files) and `interop.txt`; the harness `circuit_kat` (121 cases).
- The interop exit criterion of ADR 0011 met: the `sha256` proof (GF(2^128), 98 993 terms, 6657 inputs) and the `ecdsa2` proof (P-256, 48 256 terms, 1042 inputs) accepted end to end in about a second each; the six copies with one altered byte refused with `Merkle_Failed`.

### Changed

- 11967 checks proved in all (10738 at 0.1.0), seven proof partitions.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-10-16

Phase A complete: a verifier of Longfellow ZK (libzk) in Ada/SPARK, from the proof string to a verdict, on the explicit circuits of the pinned specification (IETF draft-google-cfrg-libzk-03), with the two complete proofs of its Appendix B.3 accepted end to end on both fields and compared value by value with the upstream verifier (google/longfellow-zk at d5e6be7). Every SPARK check of the project is proved by GNATprove with the pinned toolchain (`gnat_native 16.1.0`, `gnatprove 16.1.0`), with no justification and no `pragma Assume`.

### Added

- Stage 1, GF(2^128) (`GF2_128`; chapter 1; ADR 0004 to 0010): `Add`, `Xtime`, `Mul` and `Square` proved Gold against the ghost Horner reference `Mul_Ref`; `Inv` a fixed Itoh-Tsujii chain, Silver, total; 41 checks at the close of the stage; 209 known-answer cases anchored on the upstream subfield basis constants, 234 with the serialisation of stage 4 and the subfield injection of stage 5.
- Stage 2, the P-256 base field (`P256_Field`; chapter 2; ADR 0012 to 0017): `Add`, `Sub`, `Neg`, `Mul`, `Square`, `From_Bytes`, `To_Bytes` proved Gold against a ghost big-integer model (`To_Int (Mul (A, B)) = To_Int (A) * To_Int (B) mod p` for all inputs), canonical form by type predicate, the Solinas reduction proved as a polynomial identity in 2^32; `Inv` Silver, total; 3694 checks project-wide at the close of the stage; 467 known-answer cases anchored on the P-256 base point.
- Stage 3, the sumcheck algebra and the symbolic verifier core (`Sumcheck_Algebra`, `Sumcheck_Expression`, `Sumcheck_Verifier`, generic over a field through `Field_Signature`, instantiated for both fields; chapter 3; ADR 0022 to 0027): `Eq`, `Bindeq`, `Bind`, `Lagrange_Basis`, `Eval_Quad`, `Eval_Circuit`, `Bind_G`, `Eval_Bound_Quad` and every `Expression` operation proved structurally Gold against ghost references transcribing the draft's formulas; the core of Section 6.7 emits the Ligero constraints from a circuit, its public inputs, the padded proof and the challenges; 6811 checks at the close of the stage; 208 cases plus the recorded upstream runs with injected challenges.
- Stage 4, the Fiat-Shamir transcript and the challenge replay (`Bytes`, `Sha256`, `Aes256`, `Transcript`, `Fisher_Yates`, `Sumcheck_Transcript`; chapter 4; ADR 0028 to 0032): the transcript of draft Section 3 with its codec proved structurally Gold against a proof-only ghost sequence of the absorbed bytes, `Nat` and `Choose` Gold, the rejection loops literal; the replay of every challenge of a proof and the reader of the sumcheck part; 7810 checks at the close of the stage; Appendix B.2 reproduced byte for byte (78 cases), NIST vectors for SHA-256 and AES-256, 91 cases in all.
- Stage 5, Ligero and the complete verifier (`Merkle`, `Ligero_Geometry`, `Lagrange`, `Ligero_Proof`, `Ligero_Verifier`, `Zk_Verifier`, `Verifier_Status`, `Proof_Levels`; chapter 5; ADR 0035 to 0043): the Merkle heap and its batch verification Gold, the geometry Gold on Section 4.2.1, the Reed-Solomon code by Lagrange interpolation at the queried columns with every inverse checked at run time, the proof reader of Section 7.4 Gold on the run-length rules, the three Ligero tests structural Gold, the complete verifier of Section 7.5 Silver with proved shape; 10738 checks; 467 cases in `ligero_kat` and 333 in `sumcheck_kat`, among them the two Appendix B.3 proofs accepted from the proof string on both fields, every intermediate value equal to the recorded upstream run, and the negative cases of ADR 0040 refused with their status.
- The didactic guide (`docs/guide/`, one chapter per stage with the verification condition table), 45 architecture decision records (`docs/adr/`), the known-answer vector generators (`tests/vectors/`, 20 files and 4 upstream recordings), the single definition of the checks (`scripts/check.sh`, `make check`) and the two workflows `verify` and `prove` (ADR 0018 to 0021, 0033, 0034, 0041, 0045).

### Changed

- Consolidation after Phase A (ADR 0042 to 0045): the Status lines of the records carry amendment notes of a fixed form; the functional contracts and ghost models of the two fields are at the proof-only assertion level, so the whole harness set runs in seconds with assertions on and the slow set of ADR 0041 is folded into `make check`; the six partition checks of `prove` are required for merging.

### Trust model

What is proved, tested and trusted is listed in the README, section "Trust model", entry by entry, with the record that justifies each entry.
