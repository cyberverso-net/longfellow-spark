# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are recorded here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/); the project does not yet follow semantic versioning in the strict sense, since its interface is the pinned specification rather than an API. Every entry names the decision records (`docs/adr/`) and the guide chapter (`docs/guide/`) that describe it; the trust model that says what is proved, tested and trusted is in the README.

- The statuses `Validity_Form_Unsupported`, `Device_Key_Form_Unsupported` and `Random_Form_Unsupported` of the witness builder (#165, a note on ADR 0086). At both versions, a `validFrom` or `validUntil` that is not tag 0 over exactly `YYYY-MM-DDThh:mm:ssZ` is refused before anything is built, with the witness array zeroed, on the proofs from the document (`--mdoc`; the witness-file path takes the witness as given). Both hash circuits compare the dates with the verifier's time as raw bytes, so a lower-case `t`, which RFC 3339 allows and ISO/IEC 18013-5 does not exclude, would admit a time up to one day after `validUntil`; a date with fractional seconds or an offset would be refused only by the circuit, after the commitment. Likewise a device key that is not a COSE_Key of exactly kty 2, crv 1, x, y in that order, with no kid or alg, alone in `deviceKeyInfo`: the one form both circuits compare byte for byte, which a document may otherwise break and fail only in the circuit. Likewise a requested item whose `random` is not a byte string of at least 16 bytes (ISO/IEC 18013-5, 9.1.2.5): the circuits do not check its type, so a text string of the same length would pass them. All 38 test documents carry their dates, their device key and their items in those forms. The synthetic vectors now carry them too, and add, at each version, one accepted document and sixteen refused for the dates, six refused for the key, two accepted and three refused for `random` (`mdoc_witness_kat`, 280 checks). The `cli` step refuses the case's document with the `T` of its `validUntil` in lower case, and with its device key's crv changed to 2.
- Chapter 9 Section 6, "Conditions for certifying the circuit": the conditions under which the version 7 pair's guarantee holds, from a reading of the generator of the registered circuits (upstream's `circuit_maker` at 0d92f80c, which regenerates them byte for byte; #88). Each condition is an assumption on the issuer (matching by content, the namespace, the types in an item), on the relying party (the docType, with issuer keys exclusive to the requested type, for the AV profile a condition on its trusted list; the issuer's key a valid point; e2 not zero, on SHA-256) or on the circuit (the SHA-256 padding, the wrapper lengths), with what breaks if it fails, how it is checked and its references; each condition on the issuer that a document shows is checked by the builder of the document path, and a second occurrence of a pattern, which it does not see, is the object of a lint for issuers. The table also states a known limit closed on the prover's side (the separators of the dates) and four completeness constraints for issuers (ES256 on P-256 only; one COSE_Key form; a digest below the order n; the version 6 order of an item's entries). The malleability of ECDSA signatures is stated, as no issue. The trust model and the register (R2) refer to it.

- The satisfiability check of the prover, `Packed_Eval.Eval_Circuit`, branched on each assertion product and on each output wire, where its header, `Zk_Prover` and ADR 0051 declared a fixed loop with one branch on the accumulated result. Both tests now accumulate into the result with a Boolean `and` on the comparison with zero; the Gold contract against `Z_Ref` is unchanged. A source-level finding of the milestone review (#63): no timing leak was measured; the binary was inspected on arm64 and x86-64. Guide chapter 7.
- The header of `Uleb128` claimed that `Read` fails exactly when no terminating byte lies within four bytes, which its postcondition did not state. The postcondition now says it: success exactly when such a byte is there, every byte before it continuing, and on failure `Long` exactly when four bytes were available. A strengthening; no contract weakened (#63).
- `scripts/remote-check.sh --text-only` looked for a proved ancestor along the first parents only, where the proved head of a merged branch is the second parent, and when it found none it compared with `main` and posted `prove` without knowing that `main` was proved (it was, by the process: only green pull requests merge). - `scripts/measure-mdoc.sh` overwrote its own arguments while reading a configuration, so the second version of a run measured a case named by a number; it passed the public inputs of the version 7 hash circuit to the version 6 one (952 for 944); and it hid a failed prover or verifier behind the filter of its output. The arguments are read once, the public inputs come from `tests/vectors/mdoc_summary.txt` per circuit, and a failed command ends the script (#63).
- The heap criterion of the memory milestone (ADR 0080) could not be reproduced from the repository: the counter and upstream's driver were outside it. `scripts/measure/` now holds `heapshim.c`, `heap_v7.rs` and the build and run commands on both sides; the performance page points there (#63).
- Texts found behind the code or ahead of the proofs at the milestone review of the memory milestone (#63, ADR 0083). Chapter 6 said the release build keeps the run-time checks, which ADR 0068 removed from the proved units; chapters 5 and 6 kept the stack working sets and the quadratic input constraint of before ADR 0047 and ADR 0056; chapter 7 claimed the parity part of the encoded rows proved for the FFTs, where it is proved for the Lagrange encoder and tested with the FFTs (ADR 0057), and the round evaluations proved without the condition on the claim; chapter 7 called the sumcheck messages linear in the wires, where they are sums of products; chapter 1 said Fiat-Shamir samples challenges in the subfield, where the subfield sampler serves the prover's random rows only; the Lean correspondence omitted the condition of upstream's end-to-end statement on the MAC key share (the transcript's answer at the two Merkle roots); the trusted base register lacked the Silver units whose agreement with the reference is tested (V5) and the completeness of the prover (V6). ADR 0052 is amended: the element-wise serialisation of the writers is proved write by write and tested as a whole, not stated as a postcondition (#113 for the proof). No code change.
- Eighteen decision records whose code was merged still read Proposed, against the rule of `docs/process.md` that a record is Accepted when the code that realises it is merged (#63): ADR 0063 and 0067 (milestones M1 and M2, closed), 0065, 0066, 0068 to 0077 (the code of M2), 0078 (the close of M2), 0079 (the migration of the specification), 0081 and 0082 (decisions in force). Each Status line now reads Accepted at the date of the merge that brought it, with its proposed date and amendment notes kept; the index follows. ADR 0055, 0058 and 0059 stay Proposed until the mDoc layer, ADR 0080 until the milestone closes. No code change.
- Chapter 8 lacked the verification conditions and the measurements of block 3 of Phase D, and still promised them "when the block closes": the closing text of 20 October never reached `main`. Section 4 now gives the proof of the block (2 547 checks of the partition, 17 269 of the project, the three kinds of check that needed attention) and Section 5 its measurements (the hash proof from 19 min 59 s to 6 min 30 s, identical byte for byte; the profile after the change). ADR 0057 points there in its Status line. No code change.
- `GF2_128.Inject` branched on each bit of its argument. The prover applies it to the subfield coordinates of private inputs, in the subfield check of `Zk_Prover`, so the branch broke the constant-time discipline of ADR 0051. It now selects each basis element with a mask built from the bit, as `Mul` does, and keeps its contract against `Inject_Ref`. A source-level finding: no timing leak was measured. ADR 0051 amended, guide chapter 7, the trust model.
- The quadratic row of `Ligero_Prover.Commit` is built in its own procedure, `Quad_Fixed_Row`, so that its witness slots are proved zero in a small context: on the proving machine, whose cores are slower than the laptop's, the stepping assertions after `Commit_Row` in `Fixed_Rows` ran out of the 120-second timeout. No contract changed.

### Trust model

The trusted base register (`docs/guide/trusted-base.md`) lists every trusted item with what depends on it and what would discharge it. A circuit file is loaded with its shape proved and its identifier checked, and trusted as to what it computes (ADR 0011). Of the three premises of upstream's Lean proofs (ADR 0064), two are checked at the load (no assertion shares its wire, none is read by the layer above) and the third, the first public input equal to 1, by the verifier and the prover on a circuit with public inputs. The completeness of the prover on the four production mDoc circuits is tested in the `cli` step of `make check`, not proved (V6). The README lists the entries.
