# Verification approach

This document explains what formal verification with SPARK buys for a zero-knowledge proof system, what it cannot buy, and how the gap is covered. It is the key to reading every package header and every entry of the [trust model](trust-model.md).

The basics are in the primer: Ada and SPARK from the beginning in [part B](00-primer-spark.md), contracts in [B3](00-primer-spark.md#b3-contracts), ghost code and the builds in [B4](00-primer-spark.md#b4-ghost-code-assertion-levels-and-builds), loop invariants in [B5](00-primer-spark.md#b5-loops-recursion-and-termination), what a verification condition says in [B6](00-primer-spark.md#b6-what-a-verification-condition-says), and proved, tested and trusted in [B7](00-primer-spark.md#b7-proved-tested-trusted); the cryptography in [part A](00-primer-cryptography.md). This page gives the project's reasons.

## The system in one paragraph

Longfellow ZK proves statements about signed credentials (an mDoc with an ECDSA signature over P-256) without revealing them. The statement is expressed as an arithmetic circuit; the prover convinces the verifier that it knows a satisfying assignment by running the sumcheck protocol over the large binary field GF(2^128), where soundness follows from the Schwartz-Zippel lemma, and by committing to its intermediate values with a Ligero-style commitment: Reed-Solomon encodings of the witness, hashed into a Merkle tree, spot-checked by the verifier on randomly chosen columns. The interactive protocol is made non-interactive with Fiat-Shamir: every verifier challenge is derived by hashing the transcript so far. Characteristic 2 suits the Boolean-heavy circuits (SHA-256, CBOR parsing): addition is xor, squaring is linear, inversion is a fixed exponentiation. The ECDSA part needs prime-field arithmetic over the P-256 base field, with multi-limb integers and carries.

## What a SPARK proof is

SPARK is a subset of Ada with a verification toolset, GNATprove ([B2](00-primer-spark.md#b2-spark-and-what-it-rules-out), [B6](00-primer-spark.md#b6-what-a-verification-condition-says)). The programmer writes contracts (preconditions, postconditions, loop invariants) and GNATprove turns the program and its contracts into verification conditions (VCs), logical statements that are handed to SMT solvers. A VC that is proved holds for every execution of the subprogram, for every input in the declared types: it is a universal statement, not a test on samples. A VC that is not proved is reported with its location and kind, and the programmer either strengthens the contracts, restructures the code, or supplies a lemma. The context of a VC in an instance of a generic includes the whole formal package: every declaration visible to the generic is among the hypotheses of every VC of the instance, so a proof that uses most of the step budget is a debt paid at the next declaration added to the formal package. The proofs above half the budget are listed in the [trusted base register](trusted-base.md), Section 8.

The project uses the assurance levels of the SPARK community: Silver means that every possible run-time error (overflow, index out of range, division by zero, uninitialised read) is proved absent; Gold means that in addition the declared functional contracts are proved. Platinum, full functional correctness of the whole system, is explicitly not a goal ([ADR 0003](../adr/0003-proof-policy-and-trust-model.md)).

## What the proofs establish, and why it matters here

**Absence of run-time errors, in all the SPARK code.** Proofs and circuits are attacker-controlled input by definition. A verifier that can be crashed, or made to read out of bounds, by a crafted proof is a vulnerability regardless of the cryptography. Silver on every line proves that no overflow, index out of range, division by zero or uninitialised read can occur, for every input that meets the preconditions of the units, with no reliance on fuzzing coverage. Three things stay outside the proof: a failed allocation, code outside SPARK, and the termination of the rejection samplers, which is probabilistic. It is not a claim of availability: an input within the bounds can still ask for much memory and time ([trust model](trust-model.md)).

**Functional correctness of field arithmetic against a reference.** A reduction bug in GF(2^128) multiplication does not crash; it silently produces a different field element, the sumcheck equations no longer mean what Schwartz-Zippel assumes, and soundness is lost without any observable failure. This is the highest value per proved line in the project. The pattern is a *ghost reference*: a second implementation, written for clarity rather than speed, marked `Ghost` so it is never compiled into the product, and used as the specification. The postcondition of the real implementation says "my result equals the reference's result". GNATprove proves that for every pair of inputs. Chapter 1 does this for the multiplication and squaring of GF(2^128) ([ADR 0005](../adr/0005-mul-ref-as-proof-target.md)); chapter 2 relates the multi-limb representation of P-256 to the integer it denotes, with hand-written lemmas ([ADR 0014](../adr/0014-p256-functional-specification-and-ghost-model.md)).

**Structural properties of the verifier.** The circuit evaluator has a proved contract that defines what "the circuit accepts" means (chapter 3); the Merkle batch verification is proved against its path discipline and node model, the geometry against its constraints, the Reed-Solomon tables and the three Ligero tests against ghost references that transcribe the reference's formulas, and the verifier core emits only terms the Ligero verifier can index (chapter 5). The circuit loader accepts only well-formed circuits: wire indices in range, each term reading only the layer below, and the premises of upstream's Lean proofs ([ADR 0064](../adr/0064-lean-circuit-premises.md), chapter 6). Everything else in the verifier appeals to these.

## What the proofs cannot establish, and what covers it instead

**The reference matches the specification.** The proof says Mul = Mul_Ref. It says nothing about whether Mul_Ref is the multiplication the draft intends: the same wrong polynomial or bit order in both would prove fine. Known-answer vectors anchored on constants produced outside this repository give independent evidence that it is ([ADR 0009](../adr/0009-known-answer-vectors.md)). They test the cases they contain; they do not prove the reference.

**Inverse correctness.** That E * Inv (E) = 1 follows from Fermat's little theorem, which SMT solvers cannot derive. Inv is Silver, and the property is tested on vectors with two independent references ([ADR 0008](../adr/0008-inv-fixed-chain-total-function.md), register row V3). On the verification path the question does not arise: every inverse the Ligero verifier uses is multiplied back and checked at run time, with a status when the check fails ([ADR 0037](../adr/0037-checked-inv-and-public-data-scope.md)).

**Soundness and zero knowledge.** These are probabilistic properties of the protocol, not contracts of a subprogram. No SPARK proof touches them; upstream proves them in Lean for a model of the verifier ([the Lean correspondence](lean-correspondence.md)). The compensating control is fidelity to the pinned specification, every step citing its section, so that the implementation is an instance of the analysed protocol and not a variant ([ADR 0002](../adr/0002-pin-the-specification.md), register row S1).

**Full absorption of the Fiat-Shamir transcript.** If one public element is omitted from the hash that derives a challenge, the prover can choose that element after seeing the challenge and forge proofs (the "Frozen Heart" class of vulnerabilities). The code that omits it is perfectly well-typed and proves fine. The control is the absorption checklist of ADR 0028, written as the sequence of calls of the replay and of the Ligero verifier, compared with the reference by reading and tested on the published proofs (chapters 4 and 5, register row S3).

**Constant-time execution.** SPARK reasons about values, not about time. Constant time is a coding discipline: no secret-dependent branches or indexing, masks instead of conditionals, fixed operation sequences. The guarantee holds only for builds with assertions disabled, because contracts and ghost code execute when assertions are on and the ghost reference branches on its operand ([ADR 0006](../adr/0006-constant-time-discipline-and-scope.md), register row S4).

**Interoperability.** That proofs produced by the upstream prover verify here, and vice versa, is tested on vectors and real proofs, never proved.

**The toolchain.** The GNAT compiler, GNATprove and the SMT solvers are trusted (register rows T1 to T3). So is the pinned specification (row R1). These are listed, not hidden.

## The ghost reference pattern, in practice

The primer's [B4.1](00-primer-spark.md#b41-ghost-code-the-specification-inside-the-program) and [B5.1](00-primer-spark.md#b51-loop-invariants) introduce what follows. A ghost function is ordinary Ada code with the aspect `Ghost`: it may be called from contracts and from other ghost code, it is checked by GNATprove like any other code, and it is removed from the executable when assertions are disabled. Writing the reference as an *expression function* (a function whose body is a single expression) lets GNATprove unfold it in proofs. A recursive reference needs a `Subprogram_Variant` to prove termination.

The implementation then mirrors the reference step by step, and the *loop invariant* states, after each iteration, exactly what the reference computes for that many steps. GNATprove reasons about a loop by induction and cannot invent the inductive hypothesis: the invariant must hold on entry (base case), be preserved by one iteration (step), and at exit imply the postcondition. When a proof fails, GNATprove says which of these failed, and that localises the bug: a base-case failure is usually an initialisation mismatch, a preservation failure usually means the invariant is too weak (the solver does not know enough inside the loop) or too strong (false). The invariant is also a cut: past it the provers know only the invariants and what the loop does not modify, so a variable the loop assigns must have its value stated in the invariant, even when the line above makes it obvious (chapter 6, Section 3.7).

## Reading a package header

Every package header states, subprogram by subprogram, its level (Silver or Gold), what the postcondition is proved against, which vectors anchor what the proof does not cover, the specification sections implemented, the decision records applied, and the scope of the constant-time claim. The [trust model](trust-model.md) gathers these into one list, where every claim is proved, tested or trusted. If a header and this document disagree, the header is wrong and should be fixed, not the other way round.

## Circuits as data

The circuit compiler (SHA-256, ECDSA, CBOR parsing into circuits) is the largest part of the upstream code. The project consumes upstream-generated circuits as validated data, so that a verified verifier is reachable before a compiler ([ADR 0011](../adr/0011-circuits-as-data.md)); circuits whose property is stated and proved are the object of the certified circuits milestone ([ADR 0091](../adr/0091-certified-circuits-milestone.md)). Two questions are kept apart. That a circuit is executed safely and as its terms say is proved here. What the circuit computes, the credential property it enforces, is not: a circuit that expresses the wrong property is executed just as faithfully. The circuit files are therefore a declared trusted input, as to their meaning (register row R2), and the conditions under which the version 7 pair's guarantee holds are listed in chapter 9 Section 6.

## What "could run in parallel" means for a proved step

The steps of the [system map](system-map.md) carry a class: sequential by the transcript, sequential by data, or could run in parallel. The third says that the work inside a step splits into parts that read and write disjoint data: the rows of the encoding, the nodes of one level of the Merkle tree (each level needs the one below it), the terms of a round's polynomial, the opened columns and their paths. It is a property of the algorithm. Neither this project nor upstream uses threads on these paths: both run on one core.

For a proved step, running in parallel would not weaken what is proved, if it were done the way SPARK allows. SPARK supports tasks under the Ravenscar and Jorvik profiles, and proves the absence of data races through ownership: each task would own its slice, and the step would keep its contract, with the same postcondition over the whole. The proof of each slice would be the proof of the loop body. That is work for the milestone M4 (the performance page); nothing in the code depends on it.

The first class cannot change: each challenge is a hash of everything before it, so the steps it marks cannot start early or overlap, in any implementation.

## Lessons of the proofs

What the proofs taught that a maintainer needs before changing a proved unit, one line each, with the chapter where it applies and its record. The chapters describe the proofs as they are; how each lesson was found is in the record, where there is one.

- **An integer product slows every step around it.** Hide it behind a ghost function opened by one lemma (`Clmul64.Prod`; chapter 1 Section 2.7; [ADR 0065](../adr/0065-carry-less-multiplication-in-gf2-128.md)).
- **A quantified predicate in an induction is hidden, and opened only in small lemmas** (`Digits_Ok`, `Bits_Eq` of `Clmul64`; chapter 1 Section 2.7; [ADR 0065](../adr/0065-carry-less-multiplication-in-gf2-128.md)).
- **The predefined `=` on a record with an array is not the logical equality.** The provers do not substitute equals through it, so a fact such as `Mul_Ref (A, B1) = Mul_Ref (A, B2)` from `B1 = B2` needs its own lemma (`Lemma_Mul_Ref_Ext`; chapter 1 Section 2.7; [ADR 0065](../adr/0065-carry-less-multiplication-in-gf2-128.md)), and the protocol code uses `Same` ([B4.4](00-primer-spark.md#b44-logical-equality-same)).
- **The provers evaluate the conversion of a literal to `Big_Integer` but treat `**` as opaque.** Write 2^64 as `Conv (Unsigned_64'Last) + 1` (`P256_Field.Two_64`; chapter 2 Section 3.2; [ADR 0014](../adr/0014-p256-functional-specification-and-ghost-model.md)).
- **Modular word arithmetic reaches the integer model only through small lemmas**, each proved once from `/`, `mod` and the no-wrap condition (`Lemma_Split`, `Lemma_Sub128`, `Lemma_Add128`, `Lemma_Ext64`; chapter 2 Section 3.3; [ADR 0014](../adr/0014-p256-functional-specification-and-ghost-model.md)).
- **A polynomial identity proves by normalisation when every weight is written out as a monomial in one base**; a recursive definition left folded is an atom the normaliser cannot see through (`Lemma_Unfold`; chapter 2; [ADR 0014](../adr/0014-p256-functional-specification-and-ghost-model.md)).
- **A large context times out even when every fact is present.** Give each step its own subprogram and postcondition, and each identity its own lemma with exactly the hypotheses it needs (`Round`, `Mont_Mul`, `Lemma_Mac_Close`, `Lemma_Mont_Final`; chapter 2 Section 3.6; [ADR 0069](../adr/0069-p256-montgomery-multiplication.md)).
- **A shift of a word reaches the integer model most easily through a 128-bit product**, whose halves are the two shifted words (`Lemma_Mul_Split` with `Lemma_Split`; chapter 2; [ADR 0069](../adr/0069-p256-montgomery-multiplication.md)).
- **GNAT 16 stops with an internal error on ghost constants declared in a `declare` block of executable code.** Put the argument in a ghost lemma instead (`Lemma_Mont_Final`; chapter 2 Section 3.6; [ADR 0069](../adr/0069-p256-montgomery-multiplication.md)).
- **An assertion at `Proof_Only` is not compiled, but its `Big_Integer` temporaries can be**, with a finaliser that runs at every call. In a compiled body a `Big_Integer` term appears only in a simple assertion; the rest goes into a ghost lemma on the limbs, and the expanded code (`-gnatG`) shows whether `master_node` objects remain (`Lemma_Add_Mod`, `Lemma_Mont_Close`; chapter 2; [ADR 0076](../adr/0076-field-operations-without-call-and-finalisation-overhead.md)).
- **A structural proof through opaque functions needs a logical equality.** The predefined `=` of a record with an array component, like the extensional `=` of SPARKlib's containers, gives no congruence; state the equalities with `Same` or `Logical_Eq` (chapter 3 Section 4.1, chapter 4 Section 3.2, chapter 7 Section 3.3 for `Round_Evals` and, with a ghost `Same_Term`, the frames of the merged terms; [ADR 0023](../adr/0023-gold-perimeter-for-protocol-code.md), [ADR 0031](../adr/0031-transcript-gold-perimeter.md)).
- **A link stated with `=` carries the wires, not the coefficient.** A consumer that computes with the coefficient of a term needs `Same` on it as well (`Lemma_Walked_Step`, `Expand` of `Packed_Circuits`; chapter 3 Section 3.6; [ADR 0080](../adr/0080-milestone-memory.md)).
- **A recursive ghost definition is hidden where it is not needed** (`Hide_Info`, opened by `Unhide_Info` in the lemmas that step it), or the provers unfold it without bound and exhaust the step budget on trivial goals (chapters 3 and 4; [ADR 0023](../adr/0023-gold-perimeter-for-protocol-code.md)).
- **`2 ** N` with a variable exponent is out of the provers' reach.** Use a ghost `Big_Integer` function (`Pow2`) or a function by cases, or let the loop that finds 2^L stop at the bound by its own condition (observed in the proofs of `Sumcheck_Algebra`, `Pow2`, `Uleb128`, `Pow128`, and `Additive_Fft.Extend`; chapters 3, 6 and 8; not in a record).
- **Frame conditions on arrays of predicated records cost hundreds of thousands of steps; parallel arrays of scalars and elements cost thousands** (observed in the proof of `Sumcheck_Verifier.Constrain_To_Be_Zero` on `Ligero_Terms`; chapter 3 Section 3.5; not in a record).
- **Reasoning about a search function inside a quantifier goes into small lemmas** with `null` bodies or one-line assertions, called where the conclusion is needed (`Find` and its lemmas; chapter 3 Section 3.4; [ADR 0026](../adr/0026-sparse-expression.md)).
- **A contract that quantifies over an unbounded type is fine for the provers and fatal for the test build**, which executes it as a loop; quantify over positions (observed in the test build of `Sumcheck_Expression`, whose postconditions run; chapter 3 Section 3.4; not in a record).
- **The Why3 session can return stale "unproved" results for unchanged checks.** Every proof starts from a clean session (`gnatprove --clean`, `scripts/check.sh`; [ADR 0033](../adr/0033-parallel-proof-partitions.md)).
- **Flow analysis does not see an array initialised by two loops or by several statements per iteration.** Zero it first (observed in the flow analysis of `Sha256`, its schedule and its output; chapter 4 Section 3.1; not in a record).
- **A computed shift amount and its mask cost the provers more than literal shifts and `mod 256`** (observed in the proof of `Sha256`; chapter 4 Section 3.1; not in a record).
- **A loop variant over a buffer needs the procedure that fills the buffer to state the fill count** (observed in the proof of `Sha256.Digest`, whose padding loop needs the postcondition of `Put`; chapter 4 Section 3.1; not in a record).
- **SPARKlib's lemma vocabulary is at the level `SPARKlib_Full`, which never executes**, and ghost code that uses it goes to that level; a postcondition with levels may hold only level associations, so it cannot mix an executable part with the model (`Transcript`; [chapter 4 Section 3.2, "Assertion levels"](04-transcript.md#assertion-levels-and-the-checks-kept-in-every-build); [ADR 0031](../adr/0031-transcript-gold-perimeter.md)).
- **A nonlinear product in a precondition weighs on every check of the subprogram**, and a product with a constant the provers cannot see, such as a formal object reached through formal packages, is nonlinear. State the condition on machine integers and keep the big integers in one lemma (observed in the proof of `Sumcheck_Transcript.Read_Sumcheck_Proof`; chapter 4 Section 3.3; not in a record).
- **Three rules of the language met in the proofs:** two `out` arguments that are components of the same array alias; `'Loop_Entry` needs an object, not a function call; a subtype constraint may not depend on a variable, so make the bound a constant of the block (observed in the proofs of the units of chapter 4; not in a record).
- **A function call on an `out` parameter inside a precondition reads it**, and flow analysis reports the parameter uninitialised: state the shapes of `out` parameters on their attributes and discriminants (observed in the proof of `Ligero_Verifier`, `Vec_Shape`; chapter 5 Section 3.4; not in a record).
- **`'Length` of an array indexed by `Natural` is a range check in a contract; `'Last` is not** (observed in the proof of `Ligero_Verifier`; chapter 5; not in a record).
- **An `out` record with a dynamic predicate is initialised by one whole-record aggregate**, since a component assignment checks the predicate on the whole object (observed in the proof of `Ligero_Verifier`; chapter 5; not in a record).
- **Array concatenation in an expression function leaves range checks the provers do not close.** Write a loop with a pointwise `Same` postcondition (observed in the proof of `Ligero_Verifier`, `Ext_Row` and `Quad_Poly`; chapter 5 Section 3.4; not in a record).
- **A product of two bounded naturals needs `Lemma_Mult_Is_Monotonic` of SPARKlib once**, with the result stated in 64-bit arithmetic; a recursive reference quantified over the columns is stepped by one lemma per row (observed in the proofs of `Ligero_Proof` and `Ligero_Verifier`, `Row * NREQ` and `Ldt_Ref`, and of `Ligero_Prover`, `I * WR + J` through the ghost `Below` and `Lemma_Witness_Row_Fits`; chapters 5 and 7; not in a record).
- **`Pos + 4 <= B'Length` overflows when `Pos` is unbounded; `Pos <= B'Length - 4` does not.** Write bounds as differences (observed in the proofs of the readers `Ligero_Proof` and `Circuit_Reader`; chapters 5 and 6; not in a record).
- **The provers do not shift an index between two quantified postconditions in a large context.** When a call's postcondition indexes the appended elements from one and the caller from the first appended position, give an instantiation lemma (observed in the proof of `Sumcheck_Verifier`, `Lemma_Terms_Below`; chapter 5 Section 3.7; not in a record).
- **A `pragma Assert` on a proof-only ghost object carries the level itself**, or the test build fails on the ghost policy (chapter 5; [ADR 0044](../adr/0044-field-contracts-at-proof-only-level.md)).
- **Names: a parameter named `St` hides a formal package `ST`, and `Rem` is a reserved word** (observed in the compilation of the units of chapter 5; not in a record).
- **A record component declared `Integer` carries nothing of the postcondition of the function that filled it.** Give it a subtype of the range the function returns (observed in the proof of `Circuit_Reader`, `Signed_Delta` for the zigzag deltas; chapter 6 Section 3.3; not in a record).
- **A procedure that frees two allocations draws the flow warning "statement has no effect" at every exit**, since its outputs are dead after the return; calls of the `Unchecked_Deallocation` instances themselves are recognised (observed in the flow analysis of `Circuit_Reader`; chapter 6 Section 3.2; not in a record).
- **An `if` statement on a generic formal known statically draws a flow warning; an if-expression in a constant does not** (observed in the flow analysis of `Circuit_Reader.Compute_Id`, the field tag; chapter 6 Section 3.2; not in a record).
- **A discriminant that bounds an array indexed by `Index` is itself of type `Index`**, or the range check on the component cannot be proved (observed in the proof of `Ligero_Verifier`, the record `Work`; chapter 6 Section 3.4; not in a record).
- **A walk's state is one hidden predicate, not an instance of the recursive definition**: `Walked (P, J, K, C)` for "C is the cursor after K terms", with one lemma per step (`Packed_Circuits`, `Mark_Layer_P`; chapter 6 Section 3.7; [ADR 0080](../adr/0080-milestone-memory.md)).
- **A loop invariant is a cut: past it the provers know the invariants and the variables the loop does not modify, and nothing else.** A variable assigned in the loop and used past the invariant is stated in the invariant, even when its value is obvious from the line above; without it, every check that needed the link ran to the step limit (observed in the proof of `Circuit_Reader.Mark_Layer_P`, `pragma Loop_Invariant (I = First + K - 1)`; chapter 6 Section 3.7; not in a record).
- **A proof that closes on GF(2^128) can fail on P-256, where every product brings the field's contract into the context.** Write the ghost sums with the hidden wrappers of `Sumcheck_Algebra`, never the field operations; make the invariant a hidden function read through a lemma; and let it name only the circuit and the challenges, never a ghost copy of the terms, since SPARK refuses a ghost formal on a procedure that is not ghost (observed in the proof of the bound quad of `Sumcheck_Prover`; chapter 7 Section 3.3; [ADR 0071](../adr/0071-prover-reuses-the-bound-quad.md)).
- **An object moved inside a loop is restored before the iteration ends**, or ownership checking refuses the loop: write the move in a declare block (observed in the proof of `Sumcheck_Prover`, a swap of two vectors; chapter 7 Section 3.3; not in a record).
- **Two components of one heap object passed to one call make the provers lose every fact about the `in` one after the call.** Make the scratch buffer a heap object of its own (observed in the proof of `Ligero_Prover.Commit`, a scratch row and the encoded row; chapter 7 Section 3.4; not in a record).
- **A postcondition of a call whose `in out` actual is a component of a dereference may not be reusable at the call site in a large context**, where the same fact restated as an assertion right after the call is (observed in the proof of `Ligero_Prover.Commit`, `Com.Tab`; chapter 7 Section 3.4; not in a record).
- **An assertion that reaches the step limit in one instance of a generic goes into a ghost lemma with a null body**, proved once with the facts it needs (observed in the proof of `Zk_Prover`, `Lemma_Quad_Rows`, on the quad rows of the geometry; chapter 7 Section 3.7; not in a record).
- **GNAT 16.1.0 does not instantiate a contract on a formal subprogram that names the element type of a formal `Field_Signature` package**, directly or through a chain of formal packages: a `Pre` or a `Post` alone is enough, the generic compiles and its instance fails with "expected type GF2_128.Element, found private type F.Element". The shape that shows it: a generic with `with package F is new Field_Signature (<>)` and `with procedure Lemma (X, Y : F.Element) with Ghost, Pre => X = Y;`, instantiated with `Field_GF2_128.Sig` and `Field_GF2_128.Laws.Lemma_Same`. The same shape over a signature package of two formals compiles. Probably a defect of the compiler, not reported. Leave the formal without contract, as the lemmas of `Field_Signature` are: GNATprove proves the instances, where each formal is its actual with the actual's contract, and the caller rewrites what its own postcondition needs (observed in `Ligero_Prover`, the formal `Extend`, chapter 8 Section 3.3; and in `Local_Check`, the formal `Lemma_Same`, chapter 10 Section 3; [ADR 0093](../adr/0093-the-checker-model.md)).
- **A caller that declares `Always_Terminates` needs its callees' termination proved**: loop variants on their `while` loops, an explicit `Always_Terminates` on recursive subprograms (observed in the proof of `Additive_Fft`, called from `Commit_Row`; chapter 8 Section 3.1; not in a record).
- **A constant of a specification initialised by a function of the same package is computed before the body is elaborated**, and the program stops with `Program_Error` at start-up: make it a constant of the body behind a function (observed in `Additive_Fft`, the table `W_Hat`; chapter 8 Section 3.1; not in a record).
- **GNAT may refuse to instantiate a chain of generics inside a generic body, on the identity of types reached through formal packages.** One chain at library level with a run-time switch is simpler (observed in the command-line prover, `Cli_Random.Fill`; chapter 7 Section 3.2; not in a record).
- **An allocator of an array needs a value: `new T (0 .. N)` without one is not SPARK.** Allocate as zeros (observed in the proof of `Mdoc_Prover.Prove`, its two proof buffers; chapter 9; not in a record).
- **A function computed as a mask and specified as a quantifier proves in both directions with ghost code**: the quantifier carried one element at a time, and the position of the first mismatch kept as the counterexample for the other direction (observed in the proof of `Mdoc_Inputs.Sig_Pads_Match`, 384 elements; chapter 9; not in a record).
- **A caller can exclude a status only if the callee's contract says it is never returned, and a guarantee stated through a status the callee may return on another path does not prove.** State which statuses a procedure never returns, and state the guarantee through its condition (observed in the proof of `Mdoc_Prover.Prove_Mdoc`, whose zero guarantee is stated through the session's length rather than through `Transcript_Too_Long`, a status `Prove` may return; chapter 9; not in a record).
- **A constant restated by hand in a ghost lemma is a second source of truth.** Restate it from the code's own constant or check it once against it (observed in the proof of `Q256_Field`, whose lemma restated `Q_Prime` and `K_Q` in decimal, wrongly, while the code's hexadecimal constants were right; chapter 9 Section 3.5; not in a record).
- **A lemma copied from one modulus to another can reach the step limit on the new constants.** Restate the word primitives before the conclusion (observed in the proofs of `Q256_Field.Add` and `Sub`, the text of `P256_Field`'s; chapter 9 Section 3.5; not in a record).
- **A slice with variable bounds can be neither renamed nor used to constrain a constant's bounds.** Copy it into a constant whose bounds come from the slice (observed in the proof of `Mdoc_Parse`, the item's bytes and the MSO map; chapter 9 Section 3.6; not in a record).
- **A single-pass parser with an explicit stack proves when each step is a procedure with its contract and each open element maps to its frame**, rather than one loop with every invariant in one conjunction or an existential over the stack; a ghost lemma walks the table to show that the children of a closing container are closed (observed in the proof of `Cbor_Parse`, `Enter` and `Close_Top`; chapter 9 Section 3.6; not in a record).
- **A chain of steps that may fail needs every helper's postcondition to say that a failure sticks**, or the provers lose it between two calls (observed in the proof of `Mdoc_Parse`, the chain of five offsets; chapter 9 Section 3.6; not in a record).
