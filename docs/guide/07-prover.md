# Chapter 7: the prover

Phase C adds the other half of the protocol: from a circuit, its inputs and a randomness source to the proof string of Section 7.5, in the same order of operations as the upstream reference, so that the proofs it makes are the upstream prover's byte for byte. This chapter has the shape of the earlier ones: the mathematics of the prover's side, the algorithms as the reference writes them, the Ada/SPARK implementation with the constructs new to this phase explained at first use, and the honest account of what is proved, tested and trusted, with the verification condition table and the commands to reproduce it. The decisions are [ADR 0049](../adr/0049-phase-c-scope-the-prover.md) to [ADR 0053](../adr/0053-prover-vectors-and-cross-verification.md); the reference is google/longfellow-zk at d5e6be7, `docs/specs/rust/src/sumcheck/prover.rs`, `sumcheck/pad.rs`, `ligero/prover.rs`, `ligero/reed_solomon.rs`, `merkle.rs`, `zk/prover.rs`, `zk/common.rs` and the writers of `proto/src/writer/`.

## 1. The mathematics

### 1.1 The statement

A circuit of Section 6.3 is a stack of layers; the inputs are the wires of the deepest layer and each layer computes its outputs as quadratic forms of the layer below. The statement is that the outputs of layer 0 are all zero (and, where a term carries a Z assertion, that the asserted wire is zero). The prover knows every wire; the verifier knows the circuit, its identifier, the public inputs and the proof. Chapter 3 built the verifier's side: the sumcheck of Section 6 reduces the statement, layer by layer, to claims about the inputs, and the symbolic core turns those claims into linear constraints on a vector the prover has committed to with Ligero. This chapter builds what produces the proof the core consumes.

### 1.2 The sumcheck from the prover's side

For one layer, the verifier of chapter 3 holds a claim about the layer's outputs at a random point and checks, round by round over the bits of the two input indices, a polynomial of degree two in the round variable, given by its values at 0, 1 and a fixed point P2. The prover computes those values. It holds the wires of the layer below as two copies, one per hand (the two input indices h0 and h1 of a term), bound progressively to the challenges. In round r of hand h, for every term t = (g, h0, h1, k) with its current coefficient k, the two wires of the pair (the bound hand's index with its lowest bit cleared and set) give w0 and w1; the other hand's wire gives `other`; `coef = k * other`; the polynomial at 0 collects `coef * w0` over the terms whose index is even, the polynomial at P2 collects `(coef * (1 - P2)) * (w0 + P2 (w1 - w0))` over the even terms and `(coef * P2) * (w0 + P2 (w1 - w0))` over the odd ones. These are `eval_round_poly` of `sumcheck/prover.rs`, transcribed in its order of operations by the ghost functions `Ev0_Ref` and `Ev2_Ref` of `Sumcheck_Prover`; the value at 1 is not emitted (the verifier derives it from the claim) and not computed. After the round the prover binds the hand's wires to the challenge c (`bind` of chapter 3: w(x) := w0 + c (w1 - w0) over pairs) and updates every term's coefficient by `1 - c` or `c` according to the parity of its index, halving the index. At the end of the layer the two bound copies hold one wire each; those two values, minus their pads, are the claims the verifier checks against the next layer.

### 1.3 One-time pads and zero knowledge

Every value the prover sends in the sumcheck is a linear function of the wires, which are secret. Section 6.5 hides them: for every round and hand the prover draws two random pads hp0, hp1 and sends `p(0) - hp0` and `p(P2) - hp1` instead of p(0) and p(P2); for every layer it draws two claim pads c0, c1 and sends the claims minus them. The pads are part of the committed witness vector, after the private inputs, three per layer plus four per round, with the product pad c0 c1 for the quadratic constraint that ties them (`generate_lqc_triples`). The verifier of chapter 3 works on the padded values symbolically: its constraints involve the pad variables and the Ligero commitment binds them. What the verifier sees of the wires is therefore a one-time padded image of linear functions of them, and what makes the construction zero-knowledge is that the pads are uniformly random and used once ([ADR 0050](../adr/0050-randomness-as-a-byte-stream.md) states the consequence of a weak source: a predictable pad lets a verifier recover the wire from the message, and does not let a prover make a false statement pass, whose soundness rests on the challenges). The theorem is the paper's and stays trusted; the layout of the pads in the witness vector is proved (`Lqc_Triples`, `Lemma_Pads_Below`), and the order in which the pads are drawn is tested against the upstream bytes.

### 1.4 The Ligero commitment from the prover's side

Section 4.3 lays out the witness vector in a tableau of NROW rows and NCOL columns. With the geometry of chapter 5 (BLOCK, DBLOCK = 2 BLOCK - 1, NREQ queries, WR = BLOCK - NREQ witnesses per row), the unencoded rows are: row 0, BLOCK random elements, for the low-degree test; row 1, DBLOCK random elements, the sum of whose witness slots is subtracted from the first of them so that the row's witness part sums to zero, for the linear test; row 2, DBLOCK random elements with the witness slots zeroed, for the quadratic test; then one row per WR witnesses, each a prefix of NREQ random elements followed by the witnesses, zero beyond the vector; then three groups of rows, x, y and z, holding the three members of each quadratic triple at the position of the triple, with the same random prefix. The prefix is what makes the opened columns reveal nothing: the verifier opens NREQ columns and each row's opened values are the Reed-Solomon encoding of a polynomial whose NREQ free coefficients are uniform. A row whose witnesses lie in the subfield GF(2^16) and whose prefix is sampled in the subfield encodes into the subfield, and the proof writes its opened values in two bytes instead of sixteen (Section 7.4); the subfield boundary of the circuit file says which rows those are.

Each row is encoded on all NCOL columns: the polynomial of degree below the row's length that interpolates the row at the first points evaluates at the other NCOL - BLOCK (or NCOL - DBLOCK) points. The first columns are the row itself; the parity columns are the Lagrange combinations of chapter 5, built here for the parity columns instead of the queried ones. Then each committed column (NCOL - DBLOCK of them, the columns beyond the double block) is hashed with a fresh 32-byte nonce into a leaf, and the Merkle heap of chapter 5 gives the root. For `sha256`, NW = 6657 inputs + 576 pads = 7233 witnesses, 213 rows of 256 columns (BLOCK 51, WR 35), 155 leaves; for `ecdsa2`, 1042 + 485 = 1527 witnesses, 65 rows (BLOCK 42, WR 26), 173 leaves.

### 1.5 The Ligero proof

After the sumcheck, the transcript holds the root, the statement and every padded value; the constraints of the core are a matrix A over the witness vector and a vector b with A w + b = 0 (the reference proves that form, "marginally more convenient"). Section 4.4: the verifier draws u_ldt (one per witness and quadratic row), alphal (one per constraint), alphaq (three per triple) and u_quad (one per quadratic row). The prover answers with four polynomials, each a combination of rows of the tableau on their systematic columns: y_ldt, the LDT row plus u_ldt times every other row, on BLOCK columns; y_dot, the linear row plus, row by row, the row times the encoding of that row's slice of a_full, on DBLOCK columns, where a_full is the vector over all slots that collects alphal-weighted coefficients of A and the alphaq terms of the triples (the same `Build_A_Full` the verifier uses); y_quad, the quadratic row plus u_quad times (z - x y) over the three rows of each triple, on DBLOCK columns. The transcript absorbs the four polynomials (y_quad as its first NREQ and its last DBLOCK - BLOCK values), chooses NREQ of the committed columns, and the proof carries those columns of every row, their nonces and the Merkle opening of Section 4.1.3. Chapter 5 is the other side of each of these equations.

## 2. The algorithms

### 2.1 Forward evaluation

`Eval_Circuit` of chapter 3 computes every wire from the inputs and reports whether the outputs of layer 0 are zero and the Z assertions hold; the prover calls it first and refuses to prove otherwise (`Not_Satisfied`). The wires buffer holds every layer's values, the inputs last; the input wires of layer j are the outputs of layer j + 1, which is segment j + 1 of the buffer (`Lemma_Segment`).

### 2.2 Sampling on a byte stream

The reference draws through one trait with one method, `Rng::bytes`, and builds every sample on it: a GF(2^128) element is 16 bytes, a P-256 element is 32 bytes little-endian rejected at or above p and drawn again, a subfield element is 2 bytes mapped on the basis, a nonce is 32 bytes. The order of consumption in `ZkProver::commit` is: the pads, layer by layer, per round hp0 of hand 0, hp1 of hand 0, hp0 of hand 1, hp1 of hand 1, then c0 and c1 (the product is computed); then the tableau rows in the order of Section 1.4, the x, y and z rows of one triple row interleaved as they are sampled though placed in three groups; then the nonces, all before any leaf is hashed. `prove` consumes no randomness. Byte identity with the shipped proofs depends on this order and nothing else.

### 2.3 The sumcheck layer

For each layer, from 0 downwards: copy the layer's terms and the input wires (segment j + 1) into one buffer per hand; draw alpha and beta and bind the output index g to the claim point (`Bind_G` of chapter 3); then for each round and each hand compute the two evaluations, subtract the two pads of the round and hand, write them to the transcript and draw the challenge, bind the hand's wires to it and update the terms; after the rounds, the two claims are the first wire of each bound copy minus the claim pads, written to the transcript. The reference clones the transcript before the sumcheck and replays the symbolic core on the original; this project's transcript has state and no copy ([ADR 0028](../adr/0028-stage-4-scope-and-challenge-replay.md)), so the prover runs on the live transcript, records every challenge it draws in the `Challenges` record of chapter 3 and draws alpha_input last, and the core runs with the challenges as input ([ADR 0022](../adr/0022-stage-3-scope-and-challenges-as-input.md)); the transcript's state after that is the one the reference has after its replay, since the same bytes were absorbed and the same draws made.

### 2.4 The tableau row by row

Allocate the commitment; build the two Lagrange tables (BLOCK and DBLOCK rows, the parity columns); for each row of Section 1.4, fill the unencoded row, encode it (the systematic part is the row, each parity column is `Lagrange.Evaluate`), write it into the tableau; then draw the nonces, hash the leaves and build the heap. The encoding costs k multiplications per parity column per row, about 2.2 million field multiplications for `sha256`; the additive FFT of Section 2.2.2 would do better at the mDoc scale and is deferred ([ADR 0049](../adr/0049-phase-c-scope-the-prover.md)).

### 2.5 The Merkle opening

`open_merkle_heap` of `merkle.rs` walks the heap from node N - 1 down to 1 and, for every node on a path from an opened leaf, pushes the digest of the child that is not on a path. `Merkle.Verify` of chapter 5 consumes exactly those digests in that order; `Merkle.Open` produces them, and its postcondition says so in `Verify`'s own model (Section 3.4).

### 2.6 The proof, and the writers

`Zk_Prover.Prove` composes `zk/prover.rs`: validate (Section 3.7), draw the pads, assemble the witness vector (the private inputs, then the pads), generate the triples, commit; reset the transcript to the label, write the root and the statement (identifier, public inputs, a zero, the term count), run the sumcheck and the core, run the Ligero proof under the fixed statement hash of `zk/common.rs`, and write the bytes with the writers of chapter 7 of the draft: the root, the sumcheck part (Section 7.3: per layer and round, hand 0 at 0, hand 1 at 0, hand 0 at P2, hand 1 at P2, then the two claims), the Ligero part (Section 7.4: the four polynomials, the nonces, the opened columns in runs of full-field and subfield elements, the path). The run-length structure of Section 7.4 is decided by position, not by value ([ADR 0051](../adr/0051-constant-time-in-the-prover.md)): the reference writer tests each opened element for subfield membership and groups the rows accordingly; this project groups the three random rows, then the witness rows wholly below the subfield boundary, then the rest, which gives the same runs because a subfield row encodes into the subfield by closure and a full-field row encodes outside it except with probability 2^-112 per element thanks to its random prefix. The shipped `sha256` proof shows the three runs the rule predicts (48, 1360 and 2000 elements); on P-256 the field is its own subfield and the reference writes one subfield run of every element at full width, which the `ecdsa2` proof shows.

## 3. The Ada/SPARK implementation

### 3.1 A generic formal for the randomness

Every unit of this phase that draws randomness is generic over the source. `Sampling` takes a field signature and a formal procedure `Fill (B : out Byte_Array)`; `Sumcheck_Prover`, `Ligero_Prover` and `Zk_Prover` take an instance of `Sampling` among their formal packages. GNATprove analyses an instance, not the generic, so a proof holds for whatever `Fill` the instance names: the library instances name `Simple_Rng.Fill`, the command-line prover's instances name a procedure that reads the operating system. Nothing in the proofs depends on the bytes being random; what is proved is absence of run-time errors and the structural contracts for any byte stream, and what the randomness buys, zero knowledge, is the paper's theorem. The sampling loops of P-256 are literal rejection loops with no cap, declared possibly non-terminating as in chapter 4 (`Always_Terminates => not Sampling_Rejects`); on GF(2^128) the loop is dead and the procedure is proved terminating.

A procedure formal rather than a stateful type: the smallest interface that matches the reference's trait, and a source with state is one instance of it. The formal has no Global aspect, so the global state of the source is whatever the actual's is; the units that call through it leave their own Global aspects to be synthesised (the generic cannot name a state it does not know), and `Zk_Prover.Prove` says so in its header.

### 3.2 Sampling, Simple_Rng and the command line

`Simple_Rng` is the linear congruential generator of the upstream reference test (`tests/test_reference.rs`): state times 6364136223846793005 plus 1442695040888963407 modulo 2^64 per byte, the byte being bits 32 to 39 of the new state. It is a SPARK library package with one abstract state, because the library-level instances of the prover units name their source at instantiation and GNATprove analyses only those ([ADR 0050](../adr/0050-randomness-as-a-byte-stream.md)); it is a test source, and `longfellow_prove` offers it only behind `--seed`. The default source of the command line is `Os_Random`, which reads `/dev/urandom` and is `SPARK_Mode (Off)`: it does input on a device file. One procedure, `Cli_Random.Fill`, reads one or the other according to whether a seed was set, so the command line has one chain of instances over it; the compiler refused to instantiate the chain inside the program's generic body, on the identity of types reached through formal packages, and a run-time switch in the source is simpler than two chains.

### 3.3 Sumcheck_Prover and the Challenges record

`Sumcheck_Prover` is generic over the transcript glue of chapter 4 (which carries the algebra and the verifier core, hence the layouts of the padded proof, the challenges and the pad variables) and over `Sampling`. `Sample_Pads` fills the pad vector in the reference's order; `Lqc_Triples` numbers the quadratic constraints of the claim pads as `Verifier_Core` emits them, proved equal to the core's `Pad_Off` layout; `Round_Evals` computes the two evaluations of a round and is proved equal to the ghost transcription of `eval_round_poly` through the logical equality `Same` of chapter 3; `Prove` runs the layers on the live transcript and fills the `Challenges` record and the padded `Sumcheck_Proof` of chapter 3. The layer's terms and wires live on the heap, one buffer per hand, swapped as hands are bound; the swap moves ownership and is written in a declare block so that no object stays moved across iterations, a rule of SPARK's ownership checking that the first attempt tripped.

The proof of `Round_Evals` taught the lesson of [ADR 0023](../adr/0023-gold-perimeter-for-protocol-code.md) a second time: the step lemmas of the ghost references were first stated with the predefined `=`, from which the provers cannot conclude the logical equality `Same` the invariant needs; stated with `Same`, the three open checks closed at once.

Since milestone M1 ([ADR 0063](../adr/0063-milestone-m1-algorithmic-parity.md)), `Prove` runs the sumcheck the way the production prover does (`prover.rs` and `hquad.rs` of upstream):

- `Round_Evals_Dense` computes the round polynomial in the dense form: each term adds k times its other-hand wire to one vector, then sums over the pairs. It is Gold against `QW_Ref`, `D0_Ref` and `D2_Ref`.
  - Since milestone M2 ([ADR 0070](../adr/0070-sumcheck-and-ligero-kernels-as-upstream.md)) it takes three products per pair, as `quad_round_poly` does: a0 = sum q0 w0, s1 = sum q1 w1 and a2 = sum dq dw. Then p (P2) = a0 + P2 (s1 - a0 - a2) + P2^2 a2.
  - `Lemma_D2_Split` proves that this equals `D2_Ref`, by induction over the pairs. The identity for one pair and the additivity come from `Ring_Laws`, restated by `Sumcheck_Algebra` on the operations of the field that the prover uses.
  - Upstream takes two products per pair: it reads s1 off the claimed sum. That rests on a protocol invariant, not on a local identity, and is left for later.
- `Bind_G_T` binds g with the eq2 table of chapter 3, one product per term instead of an eq per term.
- `Merge_Runs` merges the consecutive terms with the same hands into one. It is Gold, structurally, against `Run_Id` (the run of each term) and `Run_Sum` (the sum of a run, in order).
- `Bind_H` merges, at every round, the two corners 2i and 2i + 1 of the bound hand by affine interpolation, in place, so the terms shrink with the rounds. It is Gold, structurally, against `Rd` (where output k reads) and `Corner_K` (the interpolation).

On the mDoc hash circuit the merging cuts the terms visited by the rounds fifteen times. The reference forms stay: `Round_Evals`, `Bind_G` and `Update_Terms`. The harness runs both on every layer, every round and both hands, and checks that the round polynomials agree; the proofs stay the upstream ones byte for byte.

Since milestone M2 ([ADR 0071](../adr/0071-prover-reuses-the-bound-quad.md)), `Prove` also returns the bound quad of each layer, as upstream's `SumcheckProofAux`. `Zk_Prover` passes it to `Verifier_Core`, which no longer recomputes it for the prover: 41.1 M field products on the mDoc hash circuit become 0.3 M.

- After the last round of a layer, the terms left all have both hands at 0. The sum of their coefficients is the bound quad.
- The postcondition of `Prove`, at the level `Proof_Only`, is that this value equals `BQ_Ref` of chapter 3 for every layer. It is the precondition of `Verifier_Core`.
- The proof is an invariant on coefficients, kept round by round. For each pair of residual hands, the coefficients of the terms left equal the bound terms of the layer times the factors of the challenges drawn so far.
- `Bind_G_T`, `Merge_Runs` and `Bind_H` keep their contracts. Lemmas in a ghost package carry the invariant across each of them. Since ADR 0073 the kernels and the ghost package live in `Sumcheck_HQuad`, shared with the verifier (chapter 3); `Bind_Round` and `Prove_Layer` stay in `Sumcheck_Prover`.
- The work of a layer is split so that each proof has a small context. `Merge_Terms` establishes the invariant, `Bind_Round` keeps it across one hand of one round, `Prove_Layer` runs a layer and returns its bound quad.
- Beyond the size of the eq tables, the prover computes the bound quad with `Eval_Bound_Quad`, as the verifier does.
- `prover_kat` checks, on every layer of every test circuit, that the prover's bound quad equals that of `Bound_Quads`.

The first proof closed on GF(2^128) and not on P-256, where every product brings the field's contract into the context. Three rules made it close:

- The ghost sums use the hidden wrappers of `Sumcheck_Algebra`, never the field operations.
- The invariant is a hidden function, read through a lemma.
- The invariant names only the circuit and the challenges, never a ghost copy of the terms, so it can sit in the contract of an ordinary procedure. SPARK refuses a ghost formal on a non-ghost procedure.

The lesson of `Same` came back a third time with the merged terms: the predefined `=` on a `Term` compares the coefficients with the field's `=`, which is not the logical equality. The frame invariants ("the terms not read yet are untouched") are stated with a ghost `Same_Term`.

### 3.4 Ligero_Prover: the tableau on the heap, the encoder, the opening

`Ligero_Prover` is generic over the Ligero verifier of chapter 5 (which carries the transcript glue, the proof record, the Lagrange tables and the geometry) and over `Sampling`. The `Commitment` record holds the tableau (a two-dimensional array of NROW by NCOL elements), the nonces, the Merkle heap and the two Lagrange tables, in one heap allocation under the ownership discipline of [ADR 0047](../adr/0047-heap-allocation-by-ownership.md); `Prover_Fits` says which geometries the prover handles (the proof fits, every column is an evaluation point, the parity columns fit a Lagrange table of 4096 columns, the tableau fits 2^26 elements). `Encode_Row` is the Reed-Solomon encoder: the systematic part is the row and each parity column is `Lagrange.Evaluate`, both in its postcondition. `Commit` builds the tables, then the rows in three groups (`Fixed_Rows`, `Witness_Rows`, `Quad_Rows`), each row through `Commit_Row`, which encodes into a scratch buffer and writes the tableau's row, with a postcondition that the row holds the encoding and the other rows are untouched; then `Hash_Leaves` draws the nonces, hashes the leaves and builds the heap. `Prove` is Section 1.5: it reuses `Draw_Challenges`, `Build_A_Full`, `Absorb_Polynomials` and `Choose_Columns` of the verifier, so that the two sides draw and absorb the same bytes by construction, computes the four polynomials with `Axpy` and `Vaxpy` of chapter 5 on row buffers, opens the tree with `Merkle.Open` and copies the queried columns.

Three lessons of the proof of `Commit`, recorded here because they will recur. Two components of the same heap object passed to one call (a scratch row as `in`, the encoded row as `in out`) made the provers lose every fact about the first after the call: the scratch rows became separate heap objects. A postcondition of a procedure called with a component of a dereference as its `in out` actual (`Com.Tab`) was not reusable at the call site in a large context, where the same fact restated as an assertion right after the call was; the row procedures keep their postconditions and `Commit` asserts what it needs. And a product of two bounded naturals in a loop invariant (`I * WR + J`) needs its bound stated in wide arithmetic (`Long_Long_Integer`) or a lemma on `Lemma_Mult_Is_Monotonic`; the ghost function `Below` does the first, `Lemma_Witness_Row_Fits` and `Lemma_Quad_Row_Fits` the second.

`Merkle.Open` lives in `Merkle` of chapter 5, with the digests only. Its postcondition is `Verify`'s model: the count of digests is `Missing_Before (0, N, Path_Flags (N, Indices))`, the number `Verify` consumes, and for every node on a path each child `Verify` reads, through the ghost `Child`, is the heap's node; two lemmas, `Lemma_Missing_Mono` and `Lemma_Reads_Below`, carry the fact that the positions already written are below the position being written.

### 3.5 The writers and their postconditions on the readers

`Proof_Writer` (block 2) is generic over the Ligero verifier and writes the three parts of the proof string. `Write_Sumcheck_Proof` permutes the pad order of chapter 3 into the order of Section 7.3; `Write_Ligero_Proof` writes the fixed part, then the runs chosen by position (`Sub_Row`, `Lead_Rows`, `Sub_Rows`, `Trail_Rows`), then the path, at the reader's offset in the buffer, and its postcondition is that the bytes satisfy `Well_Coded` of chapter 5, the reader's own rules, which is how the writer is proved to produce what the reader accepts; `Write_Proof` composes them with the root and its postcondition names the first 32 bytes. The equality `Read (Write (P)) = P` is not a theorem the generics can state, because `To_Bytes` and `From_Bytes` are formals without contracts; it follows from the proved layouts and the byte-level facts of chapters 1 and 2, and it is tested on every proof of the repository, read and rewritten identical ([ADR 0052](../adr/0052-gold-perimeter-for-the-prover.md)).

### 3.6 The subfield projection as a fixed linear map

Section 7.4 writes a subfield element as two bytes: the coordinates of the element on the basis of GF(2^16) in GF(2^128) that chapter 5 injects. The inverse of the injection on its image is linear over GF(2), so sixteen masks suffice: coordinate i is the parity of the element's low limb and the i-th mask (`GF2_128.Subfield_Mask`, `Parity`, `Subfield_Coords`). The masks are precomputed by Gaussian elimination in the generator, checked there on all 65536 subfield elements, and tested in `gf2_128_kat`; `In_Subfield` is the round trip through the injection. No linear system is solved at run time, and the projection costs the same on every element ([ADR 0051](../adr/0051-constant-time-in-the-prover.md)). `Field_Signature` gained one formal, `To_Subfield_Bytes`, realised by the projection on GF(2^128) and by `To_Bytes` on P-256.

### 3.7 Zk_Prover, Prover_Status, longfellow_prove

`Zk_Prover` is generic over the Ligero prover, the sumcheck prover over the same transcript glue, the writers, and a Boolean `Validate` that only the test project sets to False. `Prove` takes the label, the circuit identifier and subfield boundary of the file, the Ligero configuration, the circuit and the inputs, and fills a buffer of `Max_Proof_Len` bytes (the writer's length with the longest opening) with the proof of `Len` bytes, or reports a status: `Input_Length` when the inputs are not ninput elements; `Input_Not_One` when the circuit has public inputs and the first is not 1 ([ADR 0064](../adr/0064-lean-circuit-premises.md)); `Not_Satisfied` when `Eval_Circuit` says so; `Not_In_Subfield` when a witness the writer will project does not lie in GF(2^16), the witnesses of the rows wholly below the boundary (`Proof_Writer.Sub_Rows`), checked by projecting, re-injecting and comparing with the differences accumulated in one mask over the bytes, and never on P-256; `Config_Invalid` when a Lagrange denominator has no inverse (the caller checks `Config_Fits` beforehand, the precondition requires it); `Exhausted` when the transcript's generator passed its bound ([ADR 0043](../adr/0043-exhausted-status-and-streaming-slice.md)). The first version of the subfield check refused every input below the boundary; the harness showed that with WR 4 and boundary 4 no row is wholly below it, nothing is projected and the reference's proof would be valid, so the check follows the rows the writer projects. The working set (wires, pads, witness vector, triples, the padded proof, the challenges, the constraints, the Ligero proof record) is one heap allocation, freed on every path.

`longfellow_prove` (`cli/`, `SPARK_Mode (Off)`) parses the command line, reads the circuit file and the inputs file in the format of the upstream test data (a JSON array of decimal strings; on GF(2^128) the integer's little-endian bytes are the element, on P-256 the integer is reduced modulo p, through `Ada.Numerics.Big_Numbers`), checks the configuration, selects the source and writes the proof file; exit status 0 when the proof is written, 1 on a prover status, 2 on a usage or file error, like the verifier.

### 3.8 Constant time in the prover

[ADR 0051](../adr/0051-constant-time-in-the-prover.md) lists the units. Constant time by construction: `Sumcheck_Prover` loops over the terms of a layer, a public structure, and branches on the parity of a wire index, public, and since M1 merges terms by comparing their wire indices and tests a term's constant against zero, both public; `Ligero_Prover` branches on positions, the geometry and the boundary, copies witnesses in fixed loops and multiplies them by public Lagrange coefficients; the hashes run over fixed-length data. Written with care: the run-length structure by position (Section 2.6) and the fixed projection (Section 3.6). Two branches on witness-derived values, declared: the satisfiability check and the subfield check, each a fixed loop with a mask and one branch on the result, the prover's own decision whether to produce a proof. No inversion on a secret: the inverses are those of the public Lagrange denominators. Out of the perimeter, public: the transcript and its challenges, the circuit, the public inputs, the serialised proof, the opened columns and the path. The guarantee is scoped to builds with assertions disabled, as everywhere in this project ([ADR 0006](../adr/0006-constant-time-discipline-and-scope.md)).

## 4. What the proofs guarantee

### 4.1 For all inputs

- No run-time error in any prover unit, for any circuit, inputs, configuration and byte stream, including the ownership checks on every heap object: no null or dangling dereference, every allocation freed on every path that does not return it, no aliasing.
- The lengths and offsets of the proof string: the writers write exactly `Proof_Len` bytes, the Ligero part satisfies `Well_Coded` at the reader's offset, the first 32 bytes are the root.
- The bound quad that `Prove` returns for each layer equals the verifier's reference `BQ_Ref` (ADR 0071).
- The round evaluations equal the ghost transcription of `eval_round_poly`; the systematic part of every encoded row is the row and the parity part is `Lagrange.Evaluate`; each row of the tableau receives its encoding and the other rows are untouched; the witness slots of each unencoded row hold the witnesses, the triples' values or zeros, as Section 1.4 says; the witness slots of row 2 are zero; the opening is the one `Merkle.Verify` consumes, in its model; the triples are the core's.
- The pad layout: `Witness_Count (C)` is the private inputs plus `Pad_Total (C)` (`Lemma_Pad_Shift`), and every triple lies below it.

What the proofs do not say: that the composition of the row shapes over the whole tableau holds as one postcondition of `Commit` (proved row by row, tested slot by slot, [ADR 0052](../adr/0052-gold-perimeter-for-the-prover.md) amended at block 4 with the author); that the four polynomials of the Ligero proof are the reference's sums; that the verifier accepts the proof (completeness). These are tested.

## 5. What is only tested

### 5.1 Tier 1: the two upstream proofs reproduced byte for byte

`scripts/check.sh cli` runs `longfellow_prove` on `sha256_circuit.lfc2` with `sha256_inputs.json`, label `sha256_spec_test`, rate 3, 16 queries, 256 columns and `--seed 12345`, and on `ecdsa2` with `ecdsa2_inputs.json`, `ecdsa2_spec_test`, rate 4, 16, 256 and `--seed 123456789`, and compares the files with `sha256_proof.bin` (49 936 bytes) and `ecdsa2_proof.bin` (56 268 bytes) with `cmp`: identical, from the first run of block 5. This is the exit criterion of the phase ([ADR 0049](../adr/0049-phase-c-scope-the-prover.md)): every byte the reference prover writes, from the same inputs and the same generator, this prover writes, which tests the order of every draw, every absorption and every write. The intermediate checkpoints of the earlier blocks were tested the same way: the writers rewrite every proof of the repository identical (`writer.txt`, `writer_kat`), and the Merkle roots of the two upstream proofs are reproduced from the inputs and seeds by `Commit` alone (`ligero_commit.txt`, `prover_kat`).

### 5.2 Tier 2: the independent Python references

`gen_prover_vectors.py` transcribes the reference prover piece by piece, sharing no code with the Ada: `SimpleRng`, the samplers, the pads, `eval_round_poly`, `round_poly`, `bind` and the term update (`sumcheck_prover.txt`: five cases on the Appendix B.3 circuits and the Z-assertion circuit, the pads and the padded proof of Section 7.3 byte for byte); `layout_tableau`, `commit_merkle_heap` and `open_merkle_heap` with the Lagrange matrix of `gen_ligero_vectors.py` (`ligero_commit.txt`: six cases, the root, every unencoded row against the systematic part of the tableau, one batch opening equal to the reference's and accepted by `Merkle.Verify`, one case with a subfield boundary that exercises the subfield sampler). The subfield projection's masks are derived in `gen_gf2_128_vectors.py` and checked on all 65536 subfield elements (`gf2_128_kat`). The complete reference prover was not written: the constraints of the Ligero proof come from the symbolic core, which the generators never transcribed ([ADR 0053](../adr/0053-prover-vectors-and-cross-verification.md), amended at block 5); tier 1 and completeness cover the composition.

### 5.3 Completeness, other seeds, the negative cases

`prover_kat` runs `Zk_Prover.Prove` on the small circuits with chosen seeds and `Zk_Verifier` must accept each proof (`zk_prove.txt`, six cases, 2568 to 5580 bytes); on `sha256` and `ecdsa2`, `scripts/check.sh cli` proves under another seed and under the system source and `longfellow_verify` accepts. The negative cases: `input_length`, `not_satisfied` (three circuits, one through a Z assertion), `not_in_subfield` (boundary 4 with WR 2, where the first witness row is projected and 0x6c is not in GF(2^16); boundary 4 with WR 4 is `ok`, since nothing is projected), `config_invalid` (8192 columns at rate 1, whose parity columns exceed the Lagrange table); and for each refused case the prover instance with `Validate => False` produces a proof that `Zk_Verifier` refuses (`linear_sum_failed` for the unsatisfied circuits, `merkle_failed` for the projected element). `verify_proof_file.rs` under `tests/vectors/upstream/` verifies a proof file of this project with the upstream `ZkVerifier`; the author runs it with `cargo` on the two files `scripts/check.sh cli` leaves under `cli/out/`, outside the CI, as the README there says.

### 5.4 Times

Release build (`cli/`), one core: `longfellow_prove` on `sha256` 6.3 seconds of CPU, on `ecdsa2` 5.6, of which the commitment is about 1 second and the rest the sumcheck over 98 993 and 48 256 terms, the core and the Ligero proof. The test build with assertions on (`-gnata`): the commitment of `sha256` 8.5 seconds and of `ecdsa2` 7.1 in `prover_kat` (the executable loop invariants of `Lagrange.Build` and the postcondition of `Merkle.Build`), a complete proof of an Appendix B.3 circuit 0.2 to 0.4 seconds; `prover_kat` 25 cases in about 30 seconds; the `cli` step of `make check` 1 minute 5 seconds, `make check` 1 hour 15 minutes. The additive FFT and the mDoc scale are the next frontier of these numbers ([ADR 0049](../adr/0049-phase-c-scope-the-prover.md)).

Memory, per `Commitment` on the heap: `sha256` (213 rows of 256 elements of 16 bytes) 872 KB of tableau, 417 KB of Lagrange tables, 10 KB of heap and nonces; `ecdsa2` (65 rows of 32 bytes) 532 KB, 747 KB, 11 KB. The working set of `Zk_Prover.Prove` adds the wires (one element per wire of every layer), the pads, the witness vector, the padded proof and the challenges, the constraints (`A_Bound` entries) and the Ligero proof record; all freed before `Prove` returns.

## 6. What stays trusted, and the limits

- The operating system's generator, for the zero-knowledge property of the proofs `longfellow_prove` makes without `--seed` (`/dev/urandom`, read outside SPARK). A proof made with `--seed` is reproducible and predictable, by design, for tests.
- That `Simple_Rng` is the upstream test's generator (transcribed, and confirmed by the byte identity), and that the upstream prover's proofs are the ones its test asserts.
- The zero-knowledge theorem of the padded sumcheck and of Ligero, and the completeness theorem: the proofs state structure, the vectors check acceptance.
- The constant-time discipline, by review against the list of ADR 0051, in builds with assertions disabled.
- `Storage_Error` and in-place aggregate allocation, as chapter 6: the supported scale is the available memory for one circuit, the wires, the tableau and the tables.
- The fixed statement hash of the reference, as chapter 5.

What Phase C leaves for later: the additive FFT in place of Lagrange interpolation for the mDoc scale; the circuit compiler (Phase D), after which the circuit files stop being a trusted input; the Rust cross-verification as a recurring check outside the CI.
