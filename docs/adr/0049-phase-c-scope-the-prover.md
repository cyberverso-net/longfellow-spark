# 0049 Phase C scope: the prover and its exit criterion

## Status

Proposed (2026-10-17, block 1 of Phase C)

## Date

2026-10-17

## Context

Phase B closed with a verifier that loads the circuits the upstream compiler produces and accepts the proofs the upstream prover made for them ([ADR 0046](0046-phase-b-scope-lfc2-loader-and-interop.md) to [ADR 0048](0048-circuit-vectors-and-interop-proofs.md)). The project plan's Phase C is the prover: the sumcheck prover, the Ligero commitment and proof, the zero-knowledge masking, the serialisation, with the accepted trade-off that a SPARK prover without hardware intrinsics is correct before it is fast, and the exit criterion that its proofs verify under both the SPARK verifier and the upstream one ([ADR 0011](0011-circuits-as-data.md)).

The upstream reference at d5e6be7 (`docs/specs/rust/src`) is small: `sumcheck/prover.rs` (200 lines: `eval_round_poly`, `bind`, `sumcheck_prove_layer`, `sumcheck_prove`), `sumcheck/pad.rs` (126: `CircuitPad::sample`, `LayerPad::sample`, `flatten_into`), `ligero/prover.rs` (390: the tableau rows `layout_ildt_row`, `layout_idot_row`, `layout_iquad_row`, `layout_witness_rows`, `layout_quadratic_constraint_rows`, then `commit`, `prove_compute_y_ldt`, `prove_compute_a_full`, `prove_compute_y_dot`, `prove_compute_y_quad`, `prove`), `ligero/reed_solomon.rs` (51: `encode_row` over the Lagrange matrix of all NCOL points), `zk/prover.rs` (128: `commit`, `prove`), the writers `write_sumcheck_proof`, `write_ligero_proof`, `write_zk_proof`, and `merkle.rs` (`commit_merkle_heap`, `open_merkle_heap`). Its reference test, `tests/test_reference.rs`, runs the prover on the two shipped circuits with a seeded generator (`SimpleRng`, seeds 12345 for `sha256` and 123456789 for `ecdsa2`), asserts that the proof bytes equal the shipped files `sha256_proof.bin` and `ecdsa2_proof.bin`, and verifies them.

Everything the prover needs below the protocol exists in this project: the two fields, the transcript (the prover draws the same challenges with the same calls, `Sumcheck_Transcript.Begin_Circuit`, `Begin_Layer`, `Round_Poly`, `End_Layer`, `Transcript.Choose`), `Merkle.Build`, the geometry, the Lagrange matrix (`Lagrange.Build` at chosen columns, to extend to all columns), the forward evaluator `Eval_Circuit` of stage 3 (Gold), the symbolic core that turns a padded proof into the constraints A, b and the quadratic triples (the prover needs exactly those to build the Ligero proof, as `zk/prover.rs` calls `symbolic_sumcheck_verifier_core`), the proof readers as the specification of the writers, the loader and the release project of Phase B.

## Decision

- **Scope.** A `Prover_Status` enumeration; the serialisation writers of Sections 7.3, 7.4 and 7.5; the sumcheck prover with the one-time pads of Section 6.6; the Ligero commitment of Section 4.3 (tableau, Reed-Solomon encoding of every row on all NCOL points, Merkle tree with nonces) and its batch opening (4.1.3); the Ligero proof of Section 4.4; the composition of `zk/prover.rs`; the command-line prover `longfellow_prove`; the harness `prover_kat`; a Rust cross-verification test kept with the recorders. Randomness, constant time, the Gold perimeter and the vectors have their own records ([ADR 0050](0050-randomness-as-a-byte-stream.md), [ADR 0051](0051-constant-time-in-the-prover.md), [ADR 0052](0052-gold-perimeter-for-the-prover.md), [ADR 0053](0053-prover-vectors-and-cross-verification.md)).
- **The prover follows the reference step for step**, in the order it consumes randomness and writes the transcript, because the exit criterion is byte identity. The one structural difference is forced by stage 4's transcript, a package with state and no copy ([ADR 0028](0028-stage-4-scope-and-challenge-replay.md)): where the reference runs the sumcheck prover on a clone of the transcript and then replays the symbolic verifier core on the original, this prover runs the sumcheck on the live transcript, records every challenge it draws in the stage 3 `Challenges` record, draws `alpha_input` itself, and calls `Verifier_Core` with the challenges as input ([ADR 0022](0022-stage-3-scope-and-challenges-as-input.md)); the transcript's state after that is the one the reference has after its replay, since the same bytes were absorbed and the same draws made.
- **The prover evaluates the circuit forward from the inputs** with `Eval_Circuit` of stage 3 (Gold) to obtain the wires of every layer (`eval_circuit` of `sumcheck/common.rs`: the input wires of layer j are the outputs of layer j + 1, and the outputs of layer 0 must all be zero, which is the statement); it checks that the inputs satisfy the circuit before any other work.
- **Negative cases are statuses, not exceptions**, checked in one validation step before any other work: `Input_Length` (the inputs are not `ninput` elements), `Not_Satisfied` (an output of layer 0 is not zero, or a Z assertion fails), `Not_In_Subfield` (on GF(2^128), a private input that the writer projects on GF(2^16) and that does not lie in it: the reference prover panics there, `to_subfield_bytes` of `algebra/gf2_128.rs` calls `expect("element not in subfield")` while writing the queried columns of a row it found in the subfield; the inputs projected are those of the witness rows wholly below the subfield boundary, `Proof_Writer.Sub_Rows` of them, so an input below the boundary in a row that is not wholly below it is not projected and not checked, as the reference does not panic on it, and a boundary that puts a pad in such a row is `Not_In_Subfield` too, a pad being a full-field element; on P-256 the boundary has no effect, the field is its own subfield and the status is unreachable), `Config_Invalid` (the Ligero configuration violates Section 4.2.1 or its geometry does not fit this implementation, `Ligero_Prover.Prover_Fits`, which the caller checks before calling `Prove`, whose precondition requires it, and which `Prove` itself reports when a Lagrange denominator has no inverse), `Exhausted` (added at block 5: the transcript's generator passed its bound while the Ligero challenges were drawn or the columns chosen, ADR 0028, as the verifier's status of the same name), each tied to its cause in the postcondition where the structure allows. A proof from inputs that do not satisfy the circuit is not produced at all; and whatever a prover produces from false inputs, the verifier must refuse, which the harness checks with a prover instance that skips the satisfiability check (a harness-only instance, [ADR 0053](0053-prover-vectors-and-cross-verification.md)).
- **Memory.** The witness, the pads, the tableau (NROW by NCOL elements) and the wires of every layer live on the heap under the discipline of [ADR 0047](0047-heap-allocation-by-ownership.md); chapter 7 extends the table of bytes to the prover.
- **The command-line prover** `longfellow_prove`, in the release project `cli/`: the field, the circuit file, the inputs in the format of the upstream JSON files (an array of decimal strings, as `tests/vectors/upstream/circuits/*_inputs.json`), the label, the Ligero configuration, the output file, and the randomness source of ADR 0050; exit status 0 when a proof is written, 1 on a prover status, 2 on a usage or file error.
- **Exit criterion.** With the generator of the upstream test and its seeds, the prover produces, from the two shipped circuits and inputs, bytes identical to `sha256_proof.bin` and `ecdsa2_proof.bin`; with other seeds its proofs are accepted by `longfellow_verify` and by the upstream `ZkVerifier` (the Rust test of ADR 0053, run by hand); the negative cases give their status.
- **Not now:** the additive FFT of the draft (`extend` of Section 2.2.2); the Lagrange encoding of a row costs k times NCOL multiplications and the `sha256` tableau stays under a second in the release build; the FFT returns with the mDoc scale. The tableau of `sha256` has 213 rows: WR = BLOCK - NREQ = 51 - 16 = 35 witnesses per row; NW = 6657 private inputs plus the pads, 4 logw + 3 per layer over the twelve layers, 7233 in all; NWROW = ceil(7233 / 35) = 207 witness rows; three random rows; NQT = ceil(12 / 35) = 1 quadratic row, laid out as three rows x, y, z; 3 + 207 + 3 = 213 (`LigeroGeometry::new` of `ligero/common.rs`). Performance is measured and recorded, not optimised.

Blocks: 1, this record and its four companions, the chapter 7 outline; 2, the writers; 3, the sumcheck prover and the pads; 4, the Ligero commitment; 5, the Ligero proof, the composition, the command-line prover, the harness and the cross-verification test, where the exit criterion is met; 6, chapter 7, the README, the changelog, the `prover` proof partition.

## Alternatives considered

- **A prover with its own transcript copy.** Rejected: the transcript is a package with state since ADR 0028 (the ghost model cannot be a record component in GNAT 16); recording the challenges and feeding the core, which takes them as input anyway, reproduces the reference's state without a copy.
- **Skipping the satisfiability check** and letting the verifier refuse. Rejected: a prover that spends the Ligero commitment on false inputs and emits a proof that fails is a worse tool than one that says `Not_Satisfied` at once; the harness still checks that the verifier refuses what a check-free prover would emit.
- **Starting from the FFT**, for the mDoc scale. Deferred: the sizes at hand do not need it and the Lagrange encoder exists.
- **A different randomness order from upstream**, for a simpler layout. Rejected: it forfeits the byte-for-byte criterion, the strongest test this phase can have.

## Consequences

- New units `Prover_Status`, `Proof_Writer` (generic over the algebra), `Sumcheck_Prover`, `Ligero_Prover`, `Zk_Prover` and their instances; a `prover` proof partition; `cli/longfellow_prove`; the harness `prover_kat`; a Rust test under `tests/vectors/upstream/`.
- The README trust model gains the prover: its proofs are what the verifier accepts (tested by byte identity and by the upstream verifier), and the randomness source is trusted (ADR 0050).
- The transcript is used by prover and verifier in the same process only in sequence (one transcript at a time, ADR 0031).

## Verification impact

- Provable: absence of run-time errors on any inputs, circuit and configuration, with the ownership checks; the shapes of what the prover builds (ADR 0052); the writers as inverses of the readers.
- Tested: byte identity with the two upstream proofs; acceptance by both verifiers; the negative cases.
- Trusted: the randomness source (ADR 0050); the zero-knowledge of the construction (the paper's theorem); upstream's proofs as the reference output.
- Constant time: ADR 0051.
